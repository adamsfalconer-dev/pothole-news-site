A worksheet attached to Pasadena Unified's Monday night agenda lists ten possible school consolidations. Each is written as an arrow. The school on the left is the one whose students would move; the school on the right is the one that would take them in.

They are numbered one to ten, and here they are:

1. Blair's ninth through twelfth grades to John Muir.
2. Thurgood Marshall's ninth through twelfth grades to Pasadena High.
3. Both of those to Pasadena High.
4. Blair's sixth through eighth grades to Octavia E. Butler, and McKinley's transitional kindergarten through fifth to Hamilton, Madison and Washington.
5. Norma Coombs to Webster.
6. Don Benito to Willard.
7. Don Benito and Norma Coombs to Webster.
8. Don Benito to Webster.
9. Altadena Arts to Washington.
10. Eliot and McKinley to Blair's sixth through eighth grades.

They are alternatives, not a plan, and several are mutually exclusive. Blair's high school grades go to Muir in the first and to Pasadena High in the third. Don Benito appears three times with two destinations. Blair is on both sides, losing its middle grades in the fourth and absorbing two schools into them in the tenth.

The first three are the largest. Between them they would empty the high school grades at Blair, at Thurgood Marshall, or at both. Elsewhere the same worksheet asks what the district would save on coaching stipends, transportation and equipment by "consolidating HS (4>2) extracurricular programs."

None of this is on the agenda, whose language is procedural. Item D.2 asks the board to approve putting "Information Necessary For Board Members To Make An Informed Decision On Which Schools To Close/Consolidate" inside a committee's scope. The attachments are posted publicly alongside it, and anyone who opens them finds the schools. The agenda that summarizes them does not.

## The item about the date does not contain one

Item D.3 asks the board to fold "The Date For The Board To Vote On Which Schools To Close/Consolidate" into the same scope. Its one-page attachment proposes nothing. It lists the four regular board meetings left in 2026, on September 24, October 22, November 19 and December 10, then six tentative 2027 dates through June 24.

## One of three committees has a seat for the public

Only one of the three committee shapes on offer can include a member of the public, and it is the one the attachment says takes longest to stand up. A community committee of seven to 15 people "would require a lengthier timeline before committee meetings can begin," for three reasons: the formation process under the Brown Act, California's open-meetings law; an application process; and board approval of the members at a board meeting.

The other two are the board itself: a subcommittee of two or three trustees plus the student board member, or a committee of the whole, all seven trustees plus the student member and the superintendent or a designee. Of that one the attachment says "No final decisions about closure are made while sitting as the Committee of the Whole."

That last one already exists. The board created it on August 13, and it meets at 6 p.m. Monday. Item D.1 asks the board to modify it.

## Two weeks ago the board tabled almost all of this

Monday's business is largely August 13's returning. That night the board took up five items under a heading it calls the district transformation process, among them the data sets for deciding which schools close, the date of the closure vote, and the committee. Trustee Scott Harden moved and trustee Yarma Velazquez seconded to table four. The minutes record four votes, all 7-0.

The committee item was amended instead, so that the committee of the whole "is to finish the work of defining and clarifying the work of transformation process." That carried 6-1, with trustee Jennifer Hall Lee voting no.

Fifteen speakers were heard. Twelve are recorded as opposing school consolidation, one as favoring it, and two on subjects the minutes log without a position.
