Monterey Park's City Council voted 5-0 on Wednesday night to place a housing measure on the November 3 ballot, asking voters for permission to allow homes on land the city now reserves for stores, offices and industry.

The council had no other route. Monterey Park's Land Use and Urban Design Element, the part of the general plan that says what may be built where, can only be changed by a vote of the people. The city's own attorney put it in writing in March: the element "may only be amended by voters; the City Council may not amend it without voter approval." Voters imposed that rule on themselves in 2020, when they adopted the element as Measure JJ, and the staff report for Wednesday's item says the same.

So a council that wants housing on commercial and industrial parcels has to ask, and the last day it could ask was Friday, August 7. The city's staff report says so on its face. The council acted two days ahead of that deadline, which has now passed.

## What the measure does, and what it does not do

It does not rezone anything. If voters approve it, the council may then write the actual zoning rules, and any single parcel gets the new designation only later, by a separate council vote, after public hearings. The staff report is explicit that the council is never obliged to take that next step: adopting the zoning regulations "is itself a discretionary legislative act; nothing requires the City Council to adopt them."

Under the element as it stands, opening those parcels to housing takes a vote of the people. If the measure passes, opening them takes a vote of the council instead. Every step after November sits with the five people who voted on Wednesday.

## The question

This is the ballot form the council adopted in Resolution 2026-R63, which sets out "the exact form of the question to be voted on at the election as it should appear on the ballot":

> Proposition to Create HOME Housing Overlays in Monterey Park
>
> Shall a measure amending the Monterey Park Land Use Element to keep residential neighborhoods intact through maintaining voter-approved land use restrictions and Monterey Park's local control over the state for housing decisions, continue prohibiting data centers, provide housing for residents, prevent crime/homeless encampments, attract businesses, and improve the local economy by allowing limited residential uses in underutilized industrial, commercial/office areas, requiring public input, ensuring environmental protections, protecting public safety, parks/open space, be adopted?

Eighty-two words in all, nine of them the title and 73 the question itself. HOME is the city's name for the program, which the staff report expands as Housing Overlay, Mixed Environments. The operative change sits in one clause, "allowing limited residential uses in underutilized industrial, commercial/office areas." The rest is what the city says the measure will protect or achieve. The word "zoning" appears nowhere on the ballot, and "overlay" appears only in the title, above the question a voter is actually answering.

The question also promises to "continue prohibiting data centers." Monterey Park voters already did that, in June. Measure NDC passed on June 2 with 10,388 votes to 1,372, or 88.33 percent, and banned data centers citywide.

## Who writes the arguments, and by when

The council designated Mayor Lo to write the ballot argument in favor, and adopted a separate resolution on argument procedures. That resolution sets no filing date. It says arguments "for or against" must reach the city clerk "by the deadlines established by the City Clerk," working from the county's election calendar. The staff report says the clerk will allow at least ten days for the public to inspect what is filed. No date is set. When one is, it binds both sides, the mayor included.

## Where it stands at the county

The county's published list of November measures, last revised July 31, carries one Monterey Park measure, the tax on hotel and short-term-rental stays. The housing measure is not on it, and could not be: the county last revised that list five days before the council voted. The council also asked the Board of Supervisors on Wednesday to consolidate its election with the statewide ballot, the administrative step that puts a city measure onto the county's printed ballot.
