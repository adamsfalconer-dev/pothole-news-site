The official record of how Measure RM reached South El Monte's November 3 ballot says the motion passed 3-2. Directly beneath that line, the same page names four council members voting aye and two voting no, and Mayor Gloria Olmos appears on both lists.

That record is the minutes of the council's July 21 meeting, and on Tuesday night item 7.a asks the five council members to approve it, along with the minutes of July 7. The county's election calendar has Los Angeles County beginning to mail vote-by-mail ballots on October 5, twenty days later, and official sample ballot booklets going out by October 13.

Measure RM is the City Council Reform Measure. The county's ballot question asks South El Monte voters whether to amend the municipal code "to return to five elected City Council members and a Mayor selected from among the five elected City Council members?" Today the mayor is elected separately by the voters. Under RM the council would choose one of its own five members for the job.

The motion that called the election on RM was the second of two substitute motions that night, made by Councilmember Hector Delgado and seconded by Mayor Pro Tem Rudy Bojorquez, to adopt the resolution calling for the measure, to approve the first reading of the ordinance behind it, and to let the council itself prepare the ballot arguments. The minutes record the result this way: "Motion passed 3-2, by the following vote:" The ayes are given as Delgado, Councilmember Larry Rodriguez, Bojorquez and Mayor Olmos. The nays are given as Councilmember Manuel Acosta and Mayor Olmos. Absent, none.

Six names appear across those two lines for a five-member council, under a label that counts five votes. The two lines cannot both be right, and the document does not say which one is.

The same page carries two other gaps. The first motion on the item, for which the minutes record no vote at all, is written as "Motion by Councilmember Delgado, seconded by X," with the letter X printed where a seconder's name should be. The substitute motion that came next, "by Councilmember Acosta, seconded by Mayor Olmos, to not approve," also has no vote and no disposition recorded after it.

Everywhere else the same two sets of minutes are internally consistent. On the next motion on the same item, to write the ballot argument in support of the change, the minutes record 3-2 with Delgado, Rodriguez and Bojorquez in favor and Acosta and Olmos opposed. On the motion after that, to write the argument against, all five members are recorded in favor, 5-0. In the July 7 minutes, also up for approval Tuesday, the earlier motion directing that the measure be drawn up is recorded 3-2, with Delgado, Rodriguez and Bojorquez in favor and Acosta and Olmos opposed. In its summary of the July 21 discussion, the record says Olmos went "on the record of being against the item due to the mayor role statistically not being equal in responsibilities."

And the council's other November measure, Resolution 26-057 on term limits, which the county lists as Measure CL, is recorded on the preceding page adopted 5-0, with each of the five members named once.

None of this changes what voters will see. The minutes are the city's account of the meeting, not the action taken at it. The resolution the council adopted that night went to the county, and the county's list of measures appearing on the November 3 ballot, revised August 20, carries Measure RM with its question in full. The county's election calendar set August 12 as the last day for it to receive a request from a city to withdraw or amend a measure already submitted.

What Tuesday's vote settles is whether the account stands. The minutes are prepared by the city clerk's office, and approving them is the council's own act.

One thread remains open in the record. On September 1 Olmos put an item on the council's agenda reading "Reconsider removing Measure RM from the ballot." The minutes of that September 1 meeting have not yet appeared on an agenda, so the record does not yet show what the council did with it.
