Pomona's City Council votes Monday, Sept. 14, on approving a 16-unit permanent supportive housing project, long-term homes paired with on-site services, at 2040 N. Garey Ave. The council has already committed $5,018,406, about 92 percent of the project's $5.4 million budget, approving the purchase of the prefabricated units in March and a construction contract in April.

Monday's item is the project's noticed public hearing, and nothing is approved until the council votes: staff is asking the council to take public testimony first and then adopt the approving resolution.

Each of the 16 units is a one-bedroom of roughly 201 to 268 square feet with a kitchenette and bathroom. The staff report describes the project as housing for residents experiencing homelessness, and says the goal, written into the council's own priorities, is to house 16 low-income families by June 2027. The units would sit on the same city-owned lot as the Family Stabilization Hub, the city services building that fronts North Garey and would stay in place, providing case management and support services to the tenants; the development, including its rear parking lot, spans that parcel and part of a second city-owned parcel at the rear.

For the neighbors, the details are in the staff report: 30 parking spaces at the rear of the property, two vehicle gates opening on the existing alley, security cameras throughout, a shared laundry, bike parking, a deck, and about 2,087 square feet of new landscaping. A property management company under city contract would run the site, and a live-in manager referred by Tri-City Mental Health would stay on the property. Everyone who owns or occupies property within 1,000 feet of the site was sent a hearing notice on Sept. 3.

For taxpayers, the staff report anticipates no cost to the city's General Fund. The $5,434,417 budget is $4,448,850 in Tri-City Mental Health grant money the council accepted on a 7-0 vote last November, plus $985,567 in housing-bond proceeds the council moved over from a rehabilitation project at 252 E. 4th St.

The resolution notes that Pomona's zoning code does not apply to the city's own projects, so this development never needed the design review a private builder would face; the Sept. 14 session is the noticed public hearing on the project, and it comes before the council decides whether to approve it. The resolution also skips a new environmental study, finding the project exempt as infill development on a site already covered by the environmental review of the city's 2014 general plan.

Approval would not be open-ended. Under the resolution's conditions, it lapses if construction has not started under a valid building permit within two years, unless the city grants extensions, and any major change to the approved plans would have to go to the Planning Commission.
