Glendale's City Council did not place a quarter-cent sales tax increase on the November 3 ballot at a special meeting Tuesday morning, and the two charter amendments the city had prepared alongside it were never moved.

A member moved to declare a fiscal emergency and place the tax before voters. The motion was seconded and went to a roll call. It failed. The chair said in the next sentence that the item had failed, and asked whether anyone still wanted the charter amendments on the November ballot. No one moved them, and the council adjourned.

The council had directed staff to draft the legislation five days earlier, at a special meeting on July 30. The packet in front of it Tuesday carried seven finished resolutions and ordinances.

## Why it needed every vote

Glendale does not elect its council in November. It held that election on June 2. Under the state constitution a city may move a general tax to an election other than its regular one only if the council declares a fiscal emergency, and that declaration takes a unanimous vote. The city's own resolution said so on its face.

The two acts were drafted as one. The resolution declares the emergency "requiring the placement of" the measure on the ballot, so the emergency and the tax rose and fell together.

Glendale has not posted minutes for the meeting. The next council agenda takes up minutes from June.

## What shoppers will not be paying

The measure would have added a quarter of one percent to Glendale's sales tax, 25 cents on a $100 taxable purchase, starting April 1, 2027. The city projected about $11 million a year from it, going into the General Fund, the city's unrestricted operating money. Unprocessed food, prescription medication, real estate transactions and services would have been exempt.

Glendale's rate is 10.5 percent today, of which the city itself keeps 1.75 percent. The city's own report says the county's Measure ER adds half a percentage point on October 1, which would take the rate to 11 percent. Had the city measure been placed and passed, the rate would have reached 11.25 percent in April 2027.

The problem the city said the money would address has not moved. Its own five-year forecast, quoted in the recitals of the resolution the council declined to adopt, projects General Fund deficits of $2.6 million this budget year and then $6.9 million, $13.9 million, $20.1 million and $16.1 million through 2030-31. The city says it has already cut $20.6 million and $16.7 million from spending across two budget years, which it describes as more than $37 million in reductions in a two-year period.

## The charter questions went with it

One of the two would have rewritten the city's civil service rules: a longer probationary period, more room to promote and to hire, and future department heads made at-will employees, excluding the police and fire chiefs and applying only to those appointed after November 3. The other would have reorganized the funds the city budgets and accounts through, retired procedures the staff report calls obsolete, and allowed the city to sell bonds by negotiation as well as by competitive bid.

Placing three measures on the ballot and running the outreach and education around them was estimated at about $922,000 from the General Fund, or roughly eight percent of one year of what the tax was projected to raise. After the tax failed, council members said the cost was too high without it. No motion was made on either question. One member said a charter amendment can go only on a November ballot.

## What happens now

Friday, August 7 is the last day a city clerk can file the resolution that puts a city measure on the November 3 ballot, according to the county Registrar-Recorder's calendar for this election.

Glendale's next regular council meeting is Tuesday, August 11, four days after that, and its agenda carries no item on the tax, on a ballot measure, on an election, on a fiscal emergency or on a charter amendment. No council meeting at all appears on the city's published schedule before then.

That is not the same as the question being closed. Glendale can notice a special meeting a day ahead, and it did: the agenda for Tuesday's meeting went up on August 3.
