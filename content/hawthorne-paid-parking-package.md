Parking in Hawthorne is scheduled to get a price list Tuesday night. The city council votes on a single citywide fee schedule that would charge $1 an hour at the curb on Hawthorne Boulevard, $25 a year for a household's first residential parking permit and $500 a year for its fourth, and by its own terms would take effect immediately upon adoption.

The schedule, Resolution 8626, is the money half of a paid-parking package the council takes up in one sitting. Four ordinances are listed on the same agenda for second reading, the final vote before a city law is adopted: one redraws the city's paid parking zones and lets future parking fees and citation penalties be set by resolution, one replaces the Preferential Street Parking chapter of the municipal code with a residential permit program, one creates an employer permit program, and one adds electric bicycle rules. None of it has passed yet. Tuesday is the vote.

Here is what the schedule would charge, by the reader it lands on.

For anyone who parks on Hawthorne Boulevard: $1 an hour at the curb, with a two-hour limit. The Boulevard's median spaces and three city lots, at 129th Street, 130th Street and the Jim Thorpe lot, run on a different meter: the first hour free, then 75 cents an hour through hour five, capped at $3 a day. The schedule lists enforcement hours for every one of those areas as 24 hours a day, seven days a week, alongside overnight parking bans in most of them.

For a household on a residential street: nothing changes Wednesday morning. The permit program bills nobody on adoption. Permit zones would be created later, by council resolution, after residents petition for one and the city reviews it. In a zone, the first permit costs $25 a year, and the city waives it for income-qualified households. The second costs $50, the third $200, the fourth $500. Add all four permits up and a four-car household in a permit zone would pay $775 a year. Guest permits run $1.50 for 24 hours.

For anyone who gets a ticket: whatever the base fine, the schedule adds $40 if it goes unpaid for more than 14 days, and another $44 from day 29.

For the workers and employers on the industrial corridor: the package creates three employer permit zones, two along Jack Northrop Avenue and one on Hawthorne Boulevard at 141st Street, at $55 a month per designated street space. Two employers are already set to bypass that system. On the same night's consent calendar, the council is asked to approve an agreement reserving 20 of the 285 street spaces on Jack Northrop Avenue exclusively for Hermeus Corporation for three years at $13,200 a year, which works out to $55 a month per space, exactly the permit rate. A second agreement reserves six spaces on the east side of Hawthorne Boulevard south of 141st Street, inside the segment of the third permit zone, for the Hawthorne School District at $3,600 a year, which works out to $50 a month per space, $5 under the rate. Under both agreements the employer stays out of the city's permit system entirely and handles its own enforcement and towing.

The e-bike ordinance adds a new chapter to the traffic code. According to its staff report, the fines are already on the city's citation schedule: $500 for a first offense, $750 for a second and $1,000 for a third within three years, and the mayor moved to introduce the ordinance on July 14.

Two structural changes outlast any single number. The fee schedule replaces parking charges that lived in the municipal code, and the parking ordinance authorizes the council to set parking fees, permit fees, special-event rates and citation penalties by resolution going forward, so future increases would not require amending the code. And the city's stated plan for the money, in the staff report's words: "None. Fees and citations collected will be invested in parking infrastructure, including maintenance and operation of existing parking spaces, property acquisition, and new parking structures and lots." That first word is the report's answer to fiscal impact on the city budget; the rest is where your dollar goes.
