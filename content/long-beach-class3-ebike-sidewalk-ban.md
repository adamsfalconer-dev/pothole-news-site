Starting Friday, Sept. 25, riding a Class 3 electric bike on any sidewalk in Long Beach is illegal, an infraction that carries a fine of up to $100. The City Council adopted the ban 7-0 on Aug. 18, with two members absent; Mayor Rex Richardson signed it Aug. 25, and under the ordinance's own terms it takes effect on the 31st day after his approval.

A Class 3 e-bike, under the state definition Long Beach now uses, is the fastest legal class: a pedal-assist bike whose motor keeps helping until 28 mph. Class 1 bikes stop assisting at 20 mph, and Class 2 bikes use a throttle that cuts out at 20 mph. If you are not sure which one you own, look for the label: state law has required manufacturers to put a permanent, prominently placed label with the classification number on every e-bike since 2017.

The new rule is one sentence: "No person shall ride a Class 3 electric bicycle upon any sidewalk." It is narrower than a ban on e-bikes generally. Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes can still ride on sidewalks wherever ordinary bicycles can, which in Long Beach means outside business districts and away from bridges, pedestrian underpasses and overpasses, sidewalks next to schools, churches, recreation centers, playgrounds and senior housing, and two named stretches of the downtown waterfront, all places where sidewalk riding was already barred for every bicycle.

The same ordinance does something broader: it folds all three state e-bike classes into the municipal code's definition of "bicycle." Every sidewalk restriction that already applied to a pedal bike now applies to every e-bike by definition, so a Class 2 rider in a business district is breaking the same rule a pedal cyclist would be.

For riders along the water, the section spells out that Seaside Walk, the stretch south of Ocean Boulevard known as the Boardwalk, and Bay Shore Walk both count as sidewalks. A Class 3 bike on the Boardwalk is covered by the ban.

Where sidewalk riding remains legal, the section as adopted sets a speed limit of 15 mph, drops it to 5 mph when pedestrians are present, and requires riders to yield to pedestrians and give an audible signal before passing. On-duty police officers are exempt.

The stakes fall differently depending on who you are. A commuter on a speed pedal-assist bike loses the sidewalk everywhere in the city and belongs in the street or the bike lane after Sept. 25. A parent whose teenager rides a Class 1 or 2 bike to school should know sidewalk riding next to any school building was already illegal for every class. A pedestrian on the Boardwalk gets a rule that takes the fastest bikes off it. LAist reported in May that the council moved to draft the ban amid a spike in e-bike injuries treated at Long Beach Memorial Medical Center, 168 trauma cases over three years.

One caution for anyone who checks the law before riding: as of early Monday the city's published municipal code still showed the old sidewalk rule, without the Class 3 subsection. The codifier has not yet folded the amendment in. The signed ordinance is what controls once it takes effect.
