Glendale's City Council decides tonight whether to pay about $68,700 more so that voters can vote separately on taking the city's department heads out of civil service, instead of accepting or rejecting the whole civil service package in one question.

Two of the resolutions under item 10.b are alternatives to each other, and they carry the same charter amendments. One puts three questions on the November 3 ballot. The other puts four. The staff report prices both: $700,752.94 for three questions, $769,449.42 for four. Neither figure is in this year's budget. A third path is open too: the report recommends the ballot only "if it is so inclined," and lists placing nothing as the alternative.

The extra question does not add a new subject. It divides one. Either version puts the same three subjects to voters: the city's civil service system, the charter's fund accounting rules, and the pay and terms of the City Clerk and City Treasurer. What differs is the first of those. In the three-question version, everything the city wants to change about civil service goes to voters at once. In the four-question version it splits in two: one question covering "the Rule of Three Whole Score, length of probationary periods, and promotional reallocations," and another that, in the report's words, "would pertain to removing the Department Heads from the classified service."

The report, written by City Attorney Michael J. Garcia, does not recommend one version over the other; staff "is submitting an alternative for consideration." It does not say who asked for it, what removal from the classified service would mean for any current employee, or how many jobs it reaches, and it names no department head.

## What a department head would lose

The report does not say, but the charter it would amend does. What the department-head question is really about is who can be fired, and how. Once an employee's appointment is complete, Article XXIV of the city charter says, a worker in the classified service "shall be discharged only for cause," by a written order stating the reasons, with five days to appeal to the civil service commission. "The commission's determination shall be final," it reads. An employee still serving probation does not have that protection yet.

Employees outside the classified service get none of it from the charter: no requirement of cause, no written reasons, no appeal. The charter lists who those are: elected officials, board and commission members, the city manager, the assistant city manager, the city assessor, the city tax collector and a short list of others. Department heads are not on it. Taking them off the classified rolls means changing that list, which is what the November question would do.

The same article sets the rules the other civil service changes would touch. On hiring, it requires appointment of "one of the three persons standing highest on the appropriate eligible list," except for a set of senior posts, among them the city attorney and the directors of public works and administrative services, where competition is already suspended. On probation, it caps the trial period at 12 months. The third change in that question, "promotional reallocations," is not a term the charter uses, and the report does not explain it.

## Where the money goes

Los Angeles County runs the election and bills the city for it. The county's estimate, which the report says has now been "clarified," is $660,752.94 for three questions and $729,449.42 for four, each priced at eight pages per question. The whole of the difference is the county's charge. The $40,000 staff recommends for informational advertising is the same under either option.

Whichever option the council picks, the report says three times that the money is not there. "The funds for the consolidation are not appropriated in the City's current budget for the Clerk's Office," it reads. Both options would come out of the General Fund, the city's unrestricted operating money, through the City Clerk's elections program.

## What else is on the ballot

The fund accounting question would revise the charter's accounting and fiscal administration provisions and make what the report calls miscellaneous cleanup.

The City Clerk and City Treasurer question is a week old. The council directed staff on July 21 to add it as a separate question, on the recommendation of the Charter Review Committee, a panel of 12 Glendale residents whose meetings are open and carried on GTV6. It would bar cutting either officer's pay during their term, freeze increases and decreases alike from 30 days before the nomination filing deadline through the first day of the next term, require the council to "consider (but not necessarily approve)" adjustments for the two whenever it grants them to other city executives and officers, and set a three-term limit counting only terms that begin after November 3, 2026.

Also on tonight's agenda, and separate from all of it, is Mayor Kassakhian's request that the council consider placing a quarter-cent sales tax on the same November ballot. It is listed under councilmember future agenda requests, which is a request to take something up, not a vote to put it before voters.
