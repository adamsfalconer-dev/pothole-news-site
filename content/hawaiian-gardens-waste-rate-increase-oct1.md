Trash bills in Hawaiian Gardens go up 3.26 percent on Oct. 1. The City Council voted 5-0 on Aug. 26 to approve the increase, after City Clerk Pablo Rubio told the council there had been no majority protest, meaning written objections had not come in from more than half of the affected properties. Councilmember Victor Farfan made the motion and Mayor Pro Tem Luis Roa seconded it.

The percentage is the part a resident will notice first. It is not the part that will matter longest.

The council's action does two things. It raises rates this year by what the staff report puts at 3.26 percent, the same figure for residential, multi-family and commercial customers alike, and for the industrial roll-off bins the same schedule covers. And Resolution No. 026-2026 adopts a schedule of maximum rates that stays in force until June 30, 2031, with an adjustment built into it that the staff report says would occur automatically every July 1 across that period.

Those annual adjustments run on two things: inflation, measured by the national consumer price index for the twelve months ending each March 31, and any increase in the per-ton tipping fees the Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts, or another public agency, charge to take the city's waste. The resolution sets a limit on the pair. In the words of the document, "in no event shall the rates set forth in Exhibit A be increased for either inflation (CPI) or the disposal pass-through in any single year by more than 25%," and a disposal pass-through may never exceed what it costs the city to provide the service. The staff report says any of these annual adjustments still requires 30 days' advance notice under state law, and the resolution says a disposal pass-through in particular cannot take effect until property owners have been mailed notice at least 30 days ahead.

So the vote a reader in Hawaiian Gardens should understand is not a one-year vote. It is a five-year rate ceiling with an escalator inside it, and the council's own resolution commits staff to come back each year with a report and a recommendation on whether to charge the maximum that year.

For this year the staff report says Waste Management sought only the cost-of-living adjustment, and asked for nothing to cover disposal costs.

Waste Management is new here. The city's exclusive franchise passed to it from Waste Resources, Inc. on May 1 of this year, under a reassignment the council consented to in October 2025. The company inherited the 2018 franchise agreement, and the rates it may charge are negotiated with the city and take effect only with the council's approval.

Every property in Hawaiian Gardens is required to subscribe to the franchised service and is billed by the company directly. There is no version of this where a household shops for a cheaper hauler or drops the service.

The formal objection window is closed. Under Proposition 218, the 1996 measure that governs property-related fees, the city had to mail every affected owner and directly liable tenant a notice at least 45 days before the hearing and count the written protests; if more than half the affected parcels object, the fee cannot be imposed. Hawaiian Gardens mailed its notices on July 1 and held the hearing on Aug. 26, comfortably past that minimum. The city says in all three documents that it ran the protest proceeding out of an abundance of caution, without conceding that Proposition 218 applies to trash service provided by a private company. The staff report said no written protests at all had arrived by the time the agenda was posted, and at the hearing the clerk reported the threshold had not been met. At the hearing the city attorney, the public works director and the city manager spoke, as did every member of the council, a Waste Management representative and a member of the public.

The rate tables themselves are not in the resolution's own text. They sit in an exhibit to it. The notice the city mailed on July 1 set out those proposed rates, container size by container size and pickup frequency by pickup frequency, and the resolution says the notice is what advised customers of the table it adopted. A resident who wants the figure for their own service should start with that notice.

The council's next regular meeting is Wednesday, Sept. 9.

*What's next: The new rates take effect Oct. 1. The maximum rate schedule runs through June 30, 2031, with an adjustment each July 1 that the staff report says happens automatically, subject to 30 days' advance notice.*
