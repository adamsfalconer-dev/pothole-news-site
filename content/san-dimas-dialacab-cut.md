Dial-a-Cab, the shared-ride taxi any resident can call, stops running overnight Tuesday. From September 1 it runs 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily, a change the City Council approved 5-0 on July 28, per the clerk-attested action minutes. A rider who depends on the overnight window loses those trips that day.

Around-the-clock service would have cost about $430,000 this budget year, against $290,000 for the shorter day, and the staff report says the $140,000 after-hours share would have fallen on San Dimas alone after Claremont, a partner city, cut its own hours. Keeping it, staff wrote, would leave the transit fund that pays for the program spending more than it takes in.

A March evaluation by the Pomona Valley Transportation Authority counted 1,284 after-hours trips by 41 riders over five months of 2025, 73 of them between midnight and 5 a.m. Seventy percent of riders do not own a vehicle; nearly half ride for medical trips. The senior and disabled fare in town is $2.50.

As of Thursday, the city's transportation page still tells residents the service "operates twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week."
