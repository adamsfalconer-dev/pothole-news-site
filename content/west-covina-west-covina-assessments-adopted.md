The assessment West Covina property owners pay for streetlights and street trees goes up 2 percent this year, to $62.42 for a single-family home from $61.20. It is the largest increase the city is allowed to make, and it is not close to enough.

The City Council confirmed the citywide Lighting and Maintenance District's assessments for 2026-27 at a public hearing on July 21, one of four charges it cleared that night for collection on Los Angeles County property tax bills. The district's own Engineer's Report projects $1,933,267 from assessments and $15,100 in interest for the coming year, about $1.95 million, against $2,460,244 in spending. Streetlight and related utility bills alone come to $1,660,762.

The city closes the roughly $512,000 difference two ways. It transfers $373,000 into the district from the general fund, the account that pays for most of what the city does. The remaining $138,877 comes out of the district's reserve, which the report projects will end the year at $93,214, or about two weeks of the district's spending.

Raising the assessment enough to cover the cost is not something the council can simply vote to do. Property owners approved the citywide district's rates in a mailed-ballot proceeding in July 1997, and the rates they approved carry a ceiling that rises no more than 2 percent a year. The Engineer's Report puts the relevant inflation figure above that ceiling this year, so the district takes its capped maximum and still does not cover what it spends. For a single-family home the 2 percent works out to $1.22.

Not every property pays $62.42. The district assesses by benefit unit: a condominium counts as three-quarters of a unit, apartments on a sliding scale, a mobile home space as half, commercial land at five units an acre and vacant residential land at a quarter of a unit.

The sewer charge on the same night's agenda is not changing. The council approved putting it on the county tax roll at $82.02 per unit, which the city's staff report says the council has held at that figure since 2017. A residential unit is one dwelling; for commercial property, one unit is every 667 square feet of floor area. That hearing was about how the charge is collected, not what it costs, and because the rate is not going up the city was required to notice it only by newspaper publication rather than by mailing every owner.

Two other hearings carried the same night, confirming assessments for Landscape Maintenance Districts 4, 6 and 7 and levying the Auto Plaza business improvement district's assessment, which seven dealership sites pay rather than residents. All four hearings passed unanimously.

The clerk's minutes record no oral comment on any of the four, in favor, opposed or neither. Nine residents spoke during the meeting's general public comment period, on subjects the minutes do not record.
