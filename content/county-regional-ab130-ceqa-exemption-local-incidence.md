> *Reported in July 2026 from state filings made in May and June 2026, as part of our founding archive.*

Two housing projects in this footprint have been declared exempt from the California Environmental Quality Act in the past three months, under a section of law that did not exist two years ago.

On May 12, San Gabriel filed a notice of exemption for an 80-unit residential development with 156 garage spaces and nine guest spaces, four units deed-restricted as affordable. On June 22, Monterey Park filed one for a 159-unit condominium development at 601 Potrero Grande Drive and 2100 Saturn Street, including eight live/work units and eight units restricted to very low income households.

Both rest on the same authority, though they cite it differently: Monterey Park names Public Resources Code section 21080.66; San Gabriel names the bill that created it, Assembly Bill 130.

The section is short and it is categorical. It says CEQA "does not apply to any aspect of a housing development project" — including the permits and public improvements the project needs — if the project clears all eight of its conditions: the site is 20 acres or less (four for a builder's-remedy project); it sits inside an incorporated city or a census urban area; it has been previously developed or is surrounded by urban uses on the thresholds the statute spells out; it is consistent with the general plan and zoning; it reaches at least half the density the state's housing-element rules specify for the site; it satisfies a cross-referenced set of labour and site standards in the Government Code; it demolishes no registered historic structure; and no part of it is a hotel or other transient lodging. A density bonus, a concession or a waiver of development standards cannot be used as a reason to call the project inconsistent.

The statute is not a study requirement made lighter. It removes the study.

It does add one duty. Within 14 days, the city must notify by certified mail and email every California Native American tribe traditionally and culturally affiliated with the site, inviting consultation — and the tribe then has 60 days to request that consultation. If it does not, the consultation is treated as concluded.

Monterey Park's filing says the applicant submitted an "AB 130 Eligibility Checklist" and city staff determined it met the requirements, "including those relating to project size, urbanized location, infill status, absence of statutory exclusion areas, and the lack of any historic structure demolition." San Gabriel's says the site is under 20 acres, urban infill, consistent with zoning and the general plan with density-bonus incentives and waivers, and above the 15-units-per-acre minimum.

Together the two projects are 239 homes, 12 of them deed-restricted.
