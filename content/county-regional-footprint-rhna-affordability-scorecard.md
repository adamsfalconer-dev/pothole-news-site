> *Compiled in July 2026 from state housing figures covering the 2021–2029 planning cycle, as part of our founding archive.*

Every city in California was handed a housing assignment in October 2021 and given eight years to meet it. The assignment comes in four price bands — very low income, low income, moderate, and above moderate, which is the market-rate band — and the state tracks each one separately.

Four years and nine months into that clock, the 20 cities in this footprint have reported finishing **11,473 of their 36,896 market-rate homes: 31.1%**.

They have reported finishing **288 of their 24,571 very-low-income homes: 1.2%**.

Combine very low and low income — the two bands that reach households earning under 80% of the county median — and the valley is at **1,102 units of 37,860, or 2.9%**.

## Read the reporting column first

Cities report their own progress to the state once a year, and five of the 20 here have stopped partway through the cycle. **Pomona's most recent report in the state's data is from 2021** — year one of eight. Irwindale's is 2022. Azusa's and West Covina's are 2024. City of Industry has an assignment and no reports at all.

That matters more than a footnote can carry. A city that has not reported since 2021 shows zero completions whether it has built nothing or built a thousand homes, and the state's file cannot tell the two apart. Fifteen of the 20 cities have reported through 2025, and only those fifteen rows can be read as a count of what exists.

The five italicised rows are incomplete records, not results. Pomona's is the one that matters most: Pomona has the second-largest assignment in the footprint — 2,799 very-low-income homes and 4,910 market-rate ones, behind only Ontario — and the state's file has carried nothing for it since 2021. **What Pomona has built in the last five years is not in this table and is not known from it.** The city is the place to ask.

## Among the fifteen cities that have reported through 2025

Five have completed no very-low-income units at all: Chino, Walnut, Baldwin Park, San Gabriel and Rosemead. Every one of the five has completed market-rate homes — Chino 1,585 of them.

The three furthest along on market-rate homes are Walnut, Chino and Ontario, at 74%, 67% and 63%. Between them they have completed 7,301 market-rate homes and 30 very-low-income ones. What that gap measures is not a preference — cities do not build housing, developers do, and the very-low band depends on subsidy and financing layers that this dataset does not touch and this story cannot see. It measures what got finished.

In two places the ranking inverts. Diamond Bar has completed 3.1% of its market-rate assignment and 6.5% of its very-low-income one; La Verne is at 5.1% and 5.6%. They are the only two cities in the table where the cheaper band is further along than the expensive one, and both are small assignments where a single project moves the percentage.

Rowland Heights and Hacienda Heights are unincorporated, so neither has a line here. Their assignment belongs to Los Angeles County, which reports for them in one county-wide row that is not broken out by community.

The cycle runs to October 2029. Nothing here is a final score. What it shows is that in the bands where housing is cheapest, the fifteen cities with complete records have finished 288 homes out of 24,571 — and that five more cities stopped telling the state anything at all.
