When Signal Hill's nomination period closed on Aug. 7, four city offices were on the November ballot and four people had been nominated, one for each: two City Council seats, city clerk and city treasurer. Nobody had a challenger. Twelve days later, at a special meeting that ran five minutes, the council cancelled the Nov. 3 election and appointed all four.

Elections Code section 10229 is what allows this. It lets a city with no more candidates than offices appoint the people nominated instead of running the election, and it says that if no appointment is made by the 75th day before the election, the election is held anyway. Once an appointment is made, the section bars the city clerk from accepting any write-in candidacy filed after it.

Resolution 2026-08-6962 appoints Tina L. Hansen and Keir Jones to four-year council terms, Daritza Perez as city clerk and David A. Hopper as city treasurer. Copeland moved, Woods seconded, and the three members present voted aye; Vice Mayor Honeycutt and Jones were absent. Hansen, the mayor, voted on her own appointment.

So Signal Hill will not mark a ballot for any city office this fall. As of November 2024 the city had 7,479 registered voters, and 4,980 of them turned out that month. They do have something left to say, and the first deadline for saying it is Oct. 2.

## The letter

By the city's account, on June 29 it received a certified letter from attorney Kevin Shenkman, for the Southwest Voter Registration Education Project, saying Signal Hill's at-large elections may violate the California Voting Rights Act and threatening to sue if the city did not switch to districts itself. Resolution 2026-08-6957 says the letter sought an answer by Aug. 10, that it "did not include any evidence to support a claim of a CVRA violation," and that the city "denies that its election system violates the CVRA or any other similar law." That is the city's account. The letter is posted with the Aug. 11 agenda as a scanned image.

The council agreed to switch anyway, and wrote down why. It found, the resolution says, "due to the high cost of litigation, including the potential payment of plaintiff's attorneys' fees, that the public interest is better served by initiating a process for transition to a district-based election system."

The item sat on the Aug. 11 consent calendar, the block of routine business a council approves in one motion. City Manager Tomaino and City Attorney Richardson pulled it off for its own vote, and Richardson presented it. A speaker from the floor, Pam Dutch Hughes, said she felt district elections would hurt the community and not bring it together. It passed 4-0, Jones absent.

The date bought something. Elections Code section 10010, the resolution says, gives a city that adopts a resolution of intention within 45 days of a claim, and an ordinance within 90 days after that or as the parties stipulate, a delay in any lawsuit and a cap on the other side's legal fees. Signal Hill's ordinance is set for Nov. 10, and its city attorney is to tell Shenkman the city's intent by Nov. 9.

## The map

Somebody now draws the lines, and the city is asking residents to try.

Justin Levitt, a vice-president of National Demographics Corporation, gave a Sept. 2 workshop the numbers he is working from, off the adjusted 2020 Census and the 2020-2024 American Community Survey: 11,911 residents, 8,346 of them citizens of voting age. Four districts would hold roughly 2,978 people each; five would hold roughly 2,382. Whether it is four or five has not been decided.

Maps must be received by 5 p.m. on Oct. 2 to be considered at the Oct. 13 hearing. A resident can email one to districting@cityofsignalhill.org, or hand-deliver or mail it to the City Clerk at 2175 Cherry Ave. Drawing tools and the schedule are at drawsignalhill.org, and a second deadline, for maps to reach the Oct. 27 hearing, is 5 p.m. Oct. 16. The last hearing is Nov. 10, and the resolution says that is where the council adopts an ordinance "including the adoption of a district boundary map and the sequence of the district elections." The first by-district vote is in 2028.

The lines decide which neighbors vote together, and the sequence decides which of them pick a council member first. Both were settled citywide until this year.

The first hearing on district composition was held at 7 p.m. on Sept. 8. The city has not yet posted a record of it; the minutes are expected with the Sept. 22 agenda.
