The Torrance City Council is scheduled Tuesday to adopt the written findings that finalize its denial of a 20-unit senior apartment building on Hawthorne Boulevard, a project the city's Planning Commission approved and its own staff recommended it approve.

The item is on the consent calendar, which the agenda describes as items that "are considered routine and will be enacted by one motion and one vote. There will be no separate discussion of these items." A councilmember can pull an item off consent to be heard separately.

The vote itself was nearly four months ago. According to the four resolutions before the council Tuesday and the staff report that carries them, the council held a public hearing May 19, took testimony from the appellant, the applicant and the public, and voted 7-0 to uphold the appeal and deny the project.

What was denied is 20 senior citizen apartments and 1,608 square feet of commercial space on seven undeveloped, downward-sloping parcels on the east side of Hawthorne Boulevard, about 200 feet north of Rolling Hills Road, in Walteria. The application is in the name of Zeina Awad, with Mahmoud H. Awad as trustee of the Mahmoud and Nawal Awad Trust. One of the 20 units would have been reserved for very-low-income renters.

The applicant, the May staff report says, "has worked with staff over seven years, over multiple iterations". The Planning Commission continued the project indefinitely twice, in April 2024 and again in November 2025, before approving it 4 to 1 on Dec. 17, 2025. An appeal to the council was filed Jan. 2, 2026. The resolutions and Tuesday's agenda do not name the appellants; the May packet names three, a neighboring resident, a business on the adjacent commercial property, and a third.

Staff told the council to approve. In the May 19 report, submitted by the Planning Commission and Community Development Director Michelle Ramirez, staff wrote that the project qualifies as a housing development project under the state Housing Accountability Act. That law, staff wrote, confines the city's review to objective standards, the kind that can be measured. In staff's reading, the hillside overlay rules the neighbors were invoking are not that kind: staff wrote that the overlay's planning and design provisions call for interpretation, "such as considerations of neighborhood compatibility and view or privacy impacts", and concluded that "the application of the R-H Overlay provisions is superseded by the HAA". The same report recorded the appellant's position that "the HAA might not apply since this project depends on discretionary approvals, not objective standards." And it left the question where it started: "The City Council has the ultimate responsibility to review these projects and determine whether they qualify under the HAA."

On size, staff wrote that the project did not need a bonus to get where it was. The zone's base density is 27 units per acre, the city's senior housing standard allows up to 58, and 20 units on 0.57 acres works out to 35. The applicant, staff wrote, "did not opt to exercise the density bonus portion of the law but applied for the Density Bonus to utilize State concessions and waivers."

The bonus, in other words, was not being used to add units. What staff's report says it was used for is relief from particular city development standards. Staff wrote that the project was entitled to one concession based on the number of affordable units provided, and that waivers "may be requested when existing development standards would make construction of the project physically infeasible." The concession was to shrink the commercial space the zone requires. The waivers cut the setback next to the neighboring residential properties from 20 feet to 10 at the lower garage level, and cut front and side yard setbacks.

The findings the council is asked to adopt Tuesday run the other way. They say the site "is not physically suitable for the proposed use, because the density of the project is too large for the size of the property and its irregular shape", and that the project will not be compatible with surrounding land uses because "the existing uses to the north and east are single family residences and the proposed density of the project is too large".

That theme repeats. Three of the four resolutions come back to the same two things, the project's density and the single family houses to the north and east; the fourth stays on density alone. The development-permit resolution says the building "has not been designed to minimize possibly intrusive impacts on single family residential properties to the north and east, because the density is too large for the size of the property". The lot-division resolution says the site "is not physically suitable for the proposed density of development, because the density is too high for the size and configuration of the irregularly shaped property".

The access findings are separate and specific. One says the project "does not provide guardrail protection for the properties downslope of the site"; others cite "the removal of the guardrail" as a hazard in itself. The driveway comes off the right-of-way at a slope the findings call too steep to slow down for safely, and its exit is too short to leave safely. And the use, one finding says, would produce a hazard "occasioned by unusual volume or character of traffic or the congregating of large numbers of people or vehicles."

The environmental determination turns over with the outcome. In May the item carried categorical exemptions for infill development and minor land divisions. Tuesday's carries a statutory one, CEQA Guidelines section 15270(a), which the resolutions describe this way: the act "does not apply to projects that are rejected or disapproved by a public agency".

One thing the packet does not settle is its own date. These findings were meant to be adopted in June. Staff wrote that a consent item was agendized for the June 23 meeting, but that "the item was pulled from the agenda." It was rescheduled for Tuesday. All four resolutions in Tuesday's packet nonetheless still close by reciting that they were introduced and adopted at a regular meeting held on "the 23rd day of June 2026." The resolutions file was created Sept. 9, 2026.
