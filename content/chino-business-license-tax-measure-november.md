> *Reported from the Chino city council record of July 21, 2026, as part of our founding archive.*

Chino's city council voted unanimously on July 21 to call an election on a measure that would rewrite how the city taxes its businesses — and, in the same motion, to appropriate $100,000 from the current year's general fund surplus to fund the election and what the resolution calls "voter information activities."

Resolution 2026-049 puts Ordinance 2026-011 before voters on November 3. The ordinance adds a new Chapter 5.03 to the Chino municipal code "relating to modernizing city's business license tax," and by its own terms takes effect only if a majority of those voting on the measure approve it. The council placed it on the ballot under Elections Code section 9222, the provision that lets a city council submit its own ordinance to the voters.

The measure goes to voters as "The City of Chino Business License Reform and Small Business Protection Measure." The city attorney is directed to write the impartial analysis, and the mayor is authorised to prepare and file the argument in favour.

City manager Linda Reich presented, and the clerk's record has her presenting on "accountability and local control." Staff put the effective date at January 1, 2027.

One speaker at the meeting said the ballot language needs refining so the average voter can understand it.

Voting to call the election: Mayor Eunice M. Ulloa, Mayor Pro Tem Curtis Burton, Karen C. Comstock, Christopher Flores and Marc Lucio. Moved by Curtis Burton, seconded by Karen C. Comstock.
