## Friday, September 25: Long Beach's sidewalk ban on faster e-bikes takes effect

Long Beach's ordinance barring Class 3 electric bicycles, the ones that assist to 28 miles per hour,
from city sidewalks takes effect on this date. The ordinance's own Section 5 sets the effective date
at the thirty-first day after the mayor approves it, and the mayor signed it on August 25.

The prohibition is a new subsection of the municipal code and applies to Class 3 bikes only. Class 1
and Class 2 e-bikes are not covered by the new sentence. **A reader checking the published municipal
code will not find the new language there yet**: as of the paper's September 7 reading the online
code still carried the old section, and codification runs behind adoption. The ordinance governs from
today regardless of when the code catches up.

_Source: the signed copy of Long Beach Ordinance ORD-26-0022, read by the paper on September 7 and
held in its archive, whose Section 5 and Section 2 carry the effective-date mechanism and the
sidewalk prohibition respectively._

## Wednesday, September 30: Altadena landlords have to register with the county

Owners of rental units, rooms within rental units and mobilehome spaces in unincorporated Los Angeles
County, which includes Altadena, must register those units with the county and pay the annual
registration fee **by September 30 of each year** under the county's rent stabilization ordinances.
Registration is done through the county's rent registry at rentregistry.dcba.lacounty.gov.

Registration fees and late fees are assessed after the deadline. The county's Department of Consumer
and Business Affairs, which runs the registry, publishes user guides and holds sessions for owners
working through it for the first time; a first-time registrant needs the Assessor's Parcel Number for
the property.

_Source: the Los Angeles County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs' rent registry page,
which states "Submit your registration by September 30, 2026 to avoid late fees," read by the
paper on September 24._

## Thursday, October 1: two sets of utility rates take effect, in Long Beach and in Hawaiian Gardens

**Long Beach.** The water and sewer rate increases the city adopted this year take effect on this
date, and bills issued after it carry the new charges. The adopted increases are 6 percent on water
and 8 percent on sewer, which the city's own notice put at about $4.64 and $1.10 a month for an
average single-family household. A Long Beach account holder can check the first bill issued after
October 1 against those figures.

**Hawaiian Gardens.** Solid waste rates rise 3.26 percent for all customer classes on the same date,
under the resolution the council adopted on August 26. The protest window on those rates closed when
the August 26 hearing ended; the figure to check a bill against is the notice the city mailed on
July 1.

_Source: the Long Beach Utilities rate presentation, slide 20, and the utilities department's budget
page, held in the paper's archive; and Hawaiian Gardens Resolution 026-2026, section 2, also held in
the archive._

## Friday, October 2, 5:00 p.m.: Signal Hill's window for resident-drawn district maps closes

Signal Hill is drawing council districts after cancelling its November 3 election, and it is taking
maps from residents. **Public map submissions must be received by 5 p.m. on October 2 to be
considered by the council at its October 13 public hearing.** A resident can submit a map by email to
districting@cityofsignalhill.org, through the city's districting site at drawsignalhill.org, or by
hand delivery or mail to the City Clerk at 2175 Cherry Avenue. A hand-drawn map showing the
boundaries of each district is accepted.

A second window follows for residents who miss this one: maps received by 5 p.m. on October 16 are
considered at the October 27 hearing.

_Source: the City of Signal Hill's districting site, drawsignalhill.org, whose schedule page carries
both deadline hours verbatim, read by the paper on September 10; and the districting consultant's
September 2 community workshop deck, held in the paper's archive. **The paper tried to re-read the
schedule page on September 24 and could not:** the site renders its schedule through a script this
desk cannot run, so the dates above rest on the September 10 reading and the archived deck rather
than on a check made since. A resident with a map close to the deadline should confirm the hour with
the City Clerk._

## Monday, October 5: the county starts mailing vote-by-mail ballots for the November 3 election

Every registered voter in Los Angeles County is mailed a ballot, and the county begins that mailing
on this date. A voter who has moved, or who has not seen a ballot by the middle of the month, has
time to act: **the last day to register to vote in this election is October 19**, and official sample
ballot booklets go out beginning October 13.

This is a county mailing rather than a city one, and it reaches voters in every place in this
footprint that has a contest on the November 3 ballot.

_Source: the City of El Monte's 2026 election page, whose important-dates list carries "October 5,
2026 - Mailing of Vote by Mail Ballots by the County," "October 13, 2026 - Mailing of Official Sample
Ballot Booklets by the County" and "October 19, 2026 - Last day to register to vote for this
election," read by the paper today._

## What has already happened

**Thursday, September 24: Pasadena Unified's board met, and the record of it is not where a reader
would look.** This was the board's next regular meeting after August 27, at 5:00 p.m. in the Elbie J.
Hickambottom Board Room at 351 S. Hudson Avenue. **The district's video and agenda archive still
carries no 2026 entry later than August 13**, including the September 3, September 10 and now
September 24 meetings that have already been held. The paper has no account of what the board did
and is not inferring one from an agenda. A reader looking for the packet should expect the district's
own board pages rather than that archive.

**Thursday, September 24: the first pre-election campaign statements were due.** Committees that
raised or spent $500 or more between July 1 and September 19 in connection with a candidate or
measure on the November 3 ballot filed a Form 450 or Form 460 on this date. These are the filings
that show who is funding the measure campaigns in this footprint, and they are public the day they
are filed. The second pre-election statement, covering September 20 through October 17, is due
**October 22**.

**Tuesday, September 22: Claremont held its Proposition 218 hearing on sewer, sanitation and street
sweeping rates,** and **Tuesday, September 22: Rolling Hills Estates took the second reading of
Ordinance No. 764,** the rewrite of its bicycle and motorized-device rules that includes a ban on the
city's equestrian trails. **What either council did is still not on the public record, and as of today
the paper cannot even look.** Both cities publish their agendas, minutes and votes through the same
commercial meeting system, and on September 25 every page and data route the paper holds for both
cities returned a nineteen-byte error page reading "Invalid parameters!" under an HTTP success code.
The same routes answered normally for other cities in this footprint on the same day, which is how
the paper knows the failure is in those two cities' records and not in its own connection. Nothing
here is a finding about either vote. The advance stories are on the site, and each outcome will be
added to its story when a record of it exists.

**Monday, September 14: Malibu voted to lift its bar on SB 9 lot splits in the Palisades burn area.**
The council adopted the staff recommendation removing Issue No. PF4 from its Rebuild Policies, the
rule under which the city had refused since October to accept or process applications to split a lot
or add a second home under state housing law inside the burn area. **The vote was 4 to 1**, with the
mayor voting no. The paper established the outcome from the meeting recording, after the city's
automatic captions posted seven days late; a fuller account waits on a second independent record of
the roll call, because the paper does not report a vote count on one instrument alone.

**Monday, September 14: Pomona held its public hearing on 16 units of city-initiated supportive
housing at 2040 N. Garey Avenue.** What the council decided is still not on the public record. The
city's meeting system carries the September 14 agenda with all 44 items and no recorded action on any
of them, and the minutes remain in draft. The council's September 21 agenda approved minutes from
June 15 and July 6, so Pomona's written record of its own meetings currently runs about two months
behind. The paper is not treating the agenda as the outcome and will report the vote when a record of

**Tuesday, September 15: Inglewood, Chino and Torrance** each had an item on the floor that this

**Wednesday, September 23: Hawaiian Gardens' council held its last regular meeting before the new

The Altadena soil remediation program has a launch the county has described only as expected this
