_A note on how this page states dates, added September 1 after the same sentence went wrong twice.
Entries here are named by their date and weekday rather than by a word like "tonight" or "tomorrow."
Those words are correct when the page is published and become false at midnight without anything
changing on the page. A date that has passed reads as a date that has passed. A relative word read a
day late reads as a live deadline._

## Thursday, September 24: the first pre-election campaign statements are due

Committees that raised or spent **$500 or more between July 1 and September 19** in connection with a
candidate or measure on the November 3, 2026 ballot file their first pre-election statement on this
date, on Form 450 or Form 460. These are the filings that show who is funding the measure campaigns
in this footprint, and they are public the day they are filed.

The second pre-election statement, covering September 20 through October 17, is due **October 22**.

_Source: the Fair Political Practices Commission filing schedule for city and county general purpose
recipient committees making contributions or independent expenditures in connection with the
November 3, 2026 election, held in the paper's archive._

## Friday, September 25: Long Beach's sidewalk ban on faster e-bikes takes effect

Long Beach's ordinance barring Class 3 electric bicycles, the ones that assist to 28 miles per hour,
from city sidewalks takes effect on this date. The ordinance's own Section 5 sets the effective date
at the thirty-first day after the mayor approves it, and the mayor signed it on August 25. A rider
who has been using the sidewalk on a Class 3 bike has until Friday.

The prohibition is a new subsection of the municipal code and applies to Class 3 bikes only. Class 1
and Class 2 e-bikes are not covered by the new sentence.

_Source: the signed copy of Long Beach Ordinance ORD-26-0022, read by the paper on September 7 and
held in its archive, whose Section 5 and Section 2 carry the effective-date mechanism and the
sidewalk prohibition respectively._

## Wednesday, September 30: Altadena landlords have to register with the county

Owners of rental units, rooms within rental units and mobilehome spaces in unincorporated Los Angeles
County, which includes Altadena, must register those units with the county and pay the annual
registration fee **by September 30 of each year** under the county's rent stabilization ordinances.
Registration is done through the county's rent registry at rentregistry.dcba.lacounty.gov.

Registration fees and late fees are assessed after the deadline. The county's Department of Consumer
and Business Affairs, which runs the registry, publishes user guides and holds sessions for owners
working through it for the first time; a first-time registrant needs the Assessor's Parcel Number for
the property.

_Source: the Los Angeles County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs' own notice to rental
property owners and its rent registry page, read by the paper on September 21._

## Thursday, October 1: two sets of utility rates take effect, in Long Beach and in Hawaiian Gardens

**Long Beach.** The water and sewer rate increases the city adopted this year take effect on this
date, and bills issued after it carry the new charges. The adopted increases are 6 percent on water
and 8 percent on sewer, which the city's own notice put at about $4.64 and $1.10 a month for an
average single-family household. A Long Beach account holder can check the first bill issued after
October 1 against those figures.

**Hawaiian Gardens.** Solid waste rates rise 3.26 percent for all customer classes on the same date,
under the resolution the council adopted on August 26.

_Source: the Long Beach Utilities rate presentation, slide 20, and the utilities department's budget
page, held in the paper's archive; and Hawaiian Gardens Resolution 026-2026, section 2, also held in
the archive._

## Friday, October 2, 5:00 p.m.: Signal Hill's window for resident-drawn district maps closes

Signal Hill is drawing council districts after cancelling its November 3 election, and it is taking
maps from residents. **Public map submissions must be received by 5 p.m. on October 2 to be
considered by the council at its October 13 public hearing.** A resident can submit a map by email to
districting@cityofsignalhill.org, through the city's districting site at drawsignalhill.org, or by
hand delivery or mail to the City Clerk at 2175 Cherry Avenue. A hand-drawn map showing the
boundaries of each district is accepted.

A second window follows for residents who miss this one: maps received by 5 p.m. on October 16 are
considered at the October 27 hearing.

_Source: the City of Signal Hill's districting site, drawsignalhill.org, whose schedule page carries
both deadline hours verbatim, read by the paper on September 10; and the districting consultant's
September 2 community workshop deck, held in the paper's archive._

**Monday, September 14: Malibu voted to lift its bar on SB 9 lot splits in the Palisades burn area.**
The council adopted the staff recommendation removing Issue No. PF4 from its Rebuild Policies, the
rule under which the city had refused since October to accept or process applications to split a lot
or add a second home under state housing law inside the burn area. **The vote was 4 to 1**, with the
mayor voting no. The paper established the outcome from the meeting recording, after the city's
automatic captions posted seven days late; a fuller account waits on a second independent record of
the roll call, because the paper does not report a vote count on one instrument alone.

**Monday, September 14: Pomona held its public hearing on 16 units of city-initiated supportive
housing at 2040 N. Garey Avenue.** What the council decided is still not on the public record. The
city's meeting system carries the September 14 agenda with all 44 items and no recorded action on any
of them, and the minutes remain in draft. The council's September 21 agenda approved minutes from
June 15 and July 6, so Pomona's written record of its own meetings currently runs about two months
behind. The paper is not treating the agenda as the outcome and will report the vote when a record of
it exists.

**Tuesday, September 15: Inglewood, Chino and Torrance each had an item on the floor** that this
paper had written about in advance, on the Section 8 administrative plan, the Dirac battery plant
appeal and the Hawthorne Boulevard senior housing denial respectively. **What each body decided is

**The Altadena soil remediation program** has a launch the county has described only as expected
