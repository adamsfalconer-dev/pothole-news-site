Entries here are named by their date and weekday rather than by a word like "tonight" or "tomorrow."
Those words are correct when the page is published and become false at midnight without anything
changing on the page, which is what happened on August 30 and again on August 31. A date that has

## Monday, September 14, 7:00 p.m.: Pomona takes public comment on 16 units of supportive housing, and decides what to do about the tree contract

**Two items on the same agenda, and one of them is a public hearing.**

**Item 20 (file 26-1634) is a public hearing on Resolution 2026-103**, approving a city-initiated
16-unit modular permanent supportive housing project at 2040 N. Garey Avenue. A public hearing is
the point in the process at which a resident can speak on the record or submit a written comment,
and it is scheduled for the 7:00 p.m. meeting.

**Item 18 (file 26-1569) asks the council to direct staff on the citywide tree maintenance
contract:** either reject all bids and re-solicit, or award a two-year contract, with three optional
one-year extensions, to Mariposa Tree Management. Pomona's tree maintenance procurement has already
drawn formal protests, which the paper has covered.

**The staff report for the Garey Avenue hearing had not posted when the paper read the agenda.** The
agenda itself is final and carries 23 items.

## Tuesday, September 22, 6:30 p.m.: the Claremont rate hearing, and the protest has to be on paper

Claremont's public hearing on proposed sewer, sanitation and street-sweeping rate increases is set
for the council's September 22 regular meeting, 6:30 p.m., in the City Council Chamber at 225 Second
Street.

**A written protest must be signed and physically received before the hearing closes.** Proposition
218 counts protests by parcel, and an emailed objection is not a protest under the statute. This is
the mechanism, not a suggestion about tone: if written protests come in from a majority of affected
parcels, the council cannot adopt the rates.

**The meeting is confirmed on the city's own calendar and the agenda has not posted yet.** Claremont
posts council agendas several days ahead, so the packet with the rate figures is the document to
watch for.

_Source: the Claremont City Council meeting calendar on the city's Legistar portal, read by the
paper on September 9, which lists the September 22 City Council meeting at 6:30 p.m. with the agenda
not yet available; and the September 8 agenda's look-ahead list of tentative items, read September
4._

## Friday, September 25: Long Beach's sidewalk ban on faster e-bikes takes effect

Long Beach's ordinance barring Class 3 electric bicycles, the ones that assist to 28 miles per hour,
from city sidewalks takes effect September 25, the 31st day after the mayor approved it on August
25. A rider on a Class 3 bike who has been using the sidewalk has until that date.

## What has already happened

**Tuesday, September 8: three councils sat.** Claremont's came back from recess, and its Proposition
218 rate hearing was **not** on that agenda, which is how the September 22 date came to be the one
that matters. Long Beach took up its water and sewer increases. Hawthorne brought four parking
ordinances up for adoption, and that meeting was the comment window on them.

**Tuesday, September 1: San Dimas Dial-a-Cab stopped running overnight.** The shared-ride taxi any
resident can call dropped from 24-hour service to a 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. schedule on that date, under
item OB1 of the July 28 council and housing authority meeting, approved 5 to 0. A rider who used it
for an early shift, a night shift, a dialysis appointment or a hospital discharge lost those trips.
Get About, the book-ahead senior and disabled service, runs weekdays 8 to 5 and Saturdays 8 to 2.

**Tuesday, September 1: Glendale's council voted to mail Proposition 218 notices** for a five-year
water rate schedule. Its hearings are set for October 7, 15, 17 and 24, which puts them past the
window this page covers.

**Tuesday, September 1: South El Monte's council took up removing a measure from the ballot.** What
the council actually did with that item is not yet on the city's published record.
