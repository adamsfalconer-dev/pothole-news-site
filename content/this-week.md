_A note on how this page states dates, added September 1 after the same sentence went wrong twice.
Entries here are named by their date and weekday rather than by a word like "tonight" or "tomorrow."
Those words are correct when the page is published and become false at midnight without anything
changing on the page, which is what happened on August 30 and again on August 31. A date that has
passed reads as a date that has passed. A relative word read a day late reads as a live deadline._

## Monday, September 14, 7:00 p.m.: Pomona takes public comment on 16 units of supportive housing, and decides what to do about the tree contract

**Two items on the same agenda, and one of them is a public hearing.**

one-year extensions, to Mariposa Tree Management. Pomona's tree maintenance procurement has already
drawn formal protests, which the paper has covered.

The meeting is confirmed and its agenda is posted.

## Monday, September 14, 5:30 p.m.: Malibu votes on lifting its bar on SB 9 lot splits in the Palisades burn area

**A settlement drops the city from a housing lawsuit only if it rescinds the policy by September 29.**

Item 7.A. asks the council to remove Issue No. PF4 from its Rebuild Policies, the one-paragraph rule
under which Malibu has refused since October to accept or process applications to split a lot or add
a second home under state housing law inside the Palisades Fire burn area. The item as written would
take effect the same evening, which means an owner who cannot get the city to take an application
today could file one Tuesday morning.

**Written comment has closed; speaking has not.** The agenda set noon Friday, September 11 as the
cutoff for written comment and audiovisual material. A resident who wants to be heard on the item
now does it with a speaker slip in the chamber or a raised hand in the Zoom webinar, three minutes
per person.

Closed session begins at 4:30 p.m. and the regular session at 5:30 p.m. at City Hall, 23825 Stuart
Ranch Road, with items in this part of the agenda heard at 6:30 p.m. or later. The meeting is
streamed at malibucity.org/video, on the city's YouTube channel and over Zoom.

_Source: the Malibu City Council regular meeting agenda and the Item 7.A. agenda report for
September 14, 2026, on the city's AgendaCenter, re-read by the paper on September 12._

## Tuesday, September 15, 2:00 p.m.: Inglewood votes on a rewritten Section 8 rulebook

**A $50 floor where the plan in force sets $0, and a new power to cut subsidies mid-contract.**

Item H-2 asks the Inglewood Housing Authority to adopt a rewritten Administrative Plan for its
Housing Choice Voucher program. It raises the minimum a household must pay each month from nothing
to $50, the ceiling federal rules allow. It writes into policy the restrictions on new vouchers the
Authority says it has already imposed. And it adds a sentence to the guarantee that currently
protects families already under contract from a lowered payment standard, allowing the Authority to
lower it anyway while it is in shortfall status. HUD designated the program a shortfall agency in
June.

**A household that would owe the new minimum can ask for a hardship exemption in writing.** The plan
requires the Authority to suspend the minimum rent from the first of the month after the request
arrives and to decide within 30 calendar days.

Four bodies meet in sequence in one open session beginning at 2:00 p.m.: the City Council, the
Successor Agency, the Housing Authority and the Parking Authority. Item H-2 sits on the Housing
Authority's calendar, third in that order.

_Source: the September 15, 2026 Inglewood City Council, Successor Agency, Housing Authority and
Parking Authority agenda and the item H-2 staff report on the city's AgendaCenter, re-read by the
paper on September 14._

## Tuesday, September 15, 6:00 p.m.: Chino's council hears the battery-plant appeal, and it is the last hearing in the city's process

**The planning commission said no in July. Staff is recommending the council say yes.**

Item 19 is the public hearing on Dirac BESS LLC's appeal of the planning commission's 3-4 denial of a
400-megawatt battery storage plant near Benson and Oaks avenues. Under Chino's zoning code the
council's decision on the appeal is the city's final one, so this is the last point at which a
resident can be heard inside the city's own process. A development agreement on the same agenda would
pay the city $3 million at building permits, $3 million at opening and $600,000 for fire and
emergency preparedness, a package staff totals at a minimum of $10.6 million.

**Three ways to comment.** In person at the Chino Senior Center, 13170 Central Ave.; by phone at
(909) 340-4280; or by email to CityClerk@CityofChino.org at least two hours before the meeting
starts.

_Source: the Chino City Council agenda for September 15, 2026 and the staff report on file 26-476,
on the city's Legistar portal, re-read by the paper on September 14._

## Tuesday, September 22, 6:30 p.m.: the Claremont rate hearing, and the protest has to be on paper

Claremont's public hearing on proposed sewer, sanitation and street-sweeping rate increases is set
for the council's September 22 regular meeting, 6:30 p.m., in the City Council Chamber at 225 Second
Street.

**A written protest must be signed and physically received before the hearing closes.** Proposition
218 counts protests by parcel, and an emailed objection is not a protest under the statute. This is
the mechanism, not a suggestion about tone: if written protests come in from a majority of affected
parcels, the council cannot adopt the rates.

**The meeting is confirmed on the city's own calendar and the agenda has not posted yet.** Claremont
posts council agendas several days ahead, so the packet with the rate figures is the document to
watch for.

_Source: the Claremont City Council meeting calendar on the city's Legistar portal, re-read by the
paper on September 10, which lists the September 22 City Council meeting at 6:30 p.m. in the Council
Chamber with the agenda not yet available._

## Friday, September 25: Long Beach's sidewalk ban on faster e-bikes takes effect

Long Beach's ordinance barring Class 3 electric bicycles, the ones that assist to 28 miles per hour,
from city sidewalks takes effect September 25, the 31st day after the mayor approved it on August

## What has already happened

**Thursday, September 10: Pasadena Unified's special board meeting was a budget filing meeting, and
no school closure was on it.** For a day after the meeting the board's agenda-and-minutes record
carried no entry for it at all, which this page reported. The entry has since posted, and the agenda
is now readable there. The board took up the district's 2025-26 unaudited actuals, certified that
financial data for filing with the Los Angeles County Office of Education, approved a classroom
expenditure waiver request, approved its GANN appropriation limit calculations and a resolution
naming who may seek disaster assistance on the district's behalf, and approved an agreement with the
City of Pasadena to use the Allendale Park baseball field during school hours. Nothing about closing
or consolidating a school was on the agenda. The board also met in closed session on two existing
lawsuits, one of them Pasadena Unified's own case against the City of Pasadena.

**Tuesday, September 8: three councils sat.** Claremont's came back from recess, and its Proposition
218 rate hearing was **not** on that agenda, which is how the September 22 date came to be the one
that matters. Long Beach took up its water and sewer increases. Hawthorne brought four parking
ordinances up for adoption, and that meeting was the comment window on them.

**Tuesday, September 1: San Dimas Dial-a-Cab stopped running overnight.** The shared-ride taxi any
resident can call dropped from 24-hour service to a 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. schedule on that date, under
item OB1 of the July 28 council and housing authority meeting, approved 5 to 0. A rider who used it
for an early shift, a night shift, a dialysis appointment or a hospital discharge lost those trips.
Get About, the book-ahead senior and disabled service, runs weekdays 8 to 5 and Saturdays 8 to 2.

**Tuesday, September 1: Glendale's council voted to mail Proposition 218 notices** for a five-year
water rate schedule. Its hearings are set for October 7, 15, 17 and 24, which puts them past the
window this page covers.

**Tuesday, September 1: South El Monte's council took up removing a measure from the ballot.** What
the council actually did with that item is not yet on the city's published record.
