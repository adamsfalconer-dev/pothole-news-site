body's agenda could not be read this week, it is left off rather than guessed at from its usual
schedule. If a meeting you expect is missing here, check that body's own site.

## Friday, August 7: two separate cutoffs, and neither one moves

- **The last day a city or the county can put a measure on the November 3 ballot.** Elections Code
  section 9222 sets it at 88 days before the election. A city that has not acted by Friday has run
  out of the ordinary route.
- **The last day to file nomination papers to run for local office** in cities holding November 3
  elections. Where an incumbent does not file, state law extends that city's window to August 12.
  Filing is with each city's own clerk, so the date and the hours are worth confirming with the clerk
  for the office you care about.

## The deadlines that are actually running out

Filing an argument for or against a measure on your November 3 ballot closes on a different day in
every city.

- **Chino: 5:30 p.m. Monday, August 10.** Arguments on the city's business license tax measure,
  Ordinance 2026-011, go to the city clerk. Rebuttals are due at 5:30 p.m. August 20. The date is the
  one printed on the clerk's own published notice of the measure.
- **South Pasadena and Diamond Bar: 5 p.m. Friday, August 14.** That is also the county registrar's
  deadline for county, school and district measures, including the Hacienda La Puente Unified
  term-limit question, the Charter Oak Unified and Valle Lindo bonds, and the countywide
  binding-arbitration charter amendment.
- **Chino Hills: 11:30 a.m. Monday, August 17.** This is the earliest hour of any deadline the paper
  has found, and it is the one most easily missed. Chino Hills votes through San Bernardino County,
  and that county's measure list carries no date and tells voters to contact the city clerk. The
  city's own record has it: the resolution on the filing of written arguments in the council's July 14
  agenda packet says arguments must be received in the clerk's office no later than 11:30 a.m. on
  August 17, and rebuttals no later than 4:30 p.m. on August 21. The standard is receipt, not
  postmark. A voter working from the county's noon and 5 p.m. hours would have the right day and be
  half an hour late. That resolution gives no street address and says nothing about mail, email or
  fax; a second resolution in the same packet gives the city clerk and election official's office as
  909-364-2620.

**Already closed:** Baldwin Park's window on its cannabis tax measure closed at 4 p.m. Wednesday,
August 5; rebuttals there are due by 4 p.m. August 17. San Gabriel's deadline on its 1 cent sales tax
measure closed at 5 p.m. Tuesday, August 4, with rebuttals due at 5 p.m. Friday, August 14. Monterey
Park's window closed July 16.

## Glendale did not put its sales tax on the ballot

- **Glendale City Council.** The paper said on this page a day ago that what the council did at its
  August 4 special meeting was not yet on a record it could read. It is now, on the city's own
  58-minute recording of the meeting. **The council did not place the quarter percent sales tax
  increase on the November 3 ballot.** A member moved to declare a fiscal emergency and put the
  measure to voters, the motion was seconded and went to a roll call, and it failed. Glendale does not
  elect its council in November, and under the state constitution a city may move a general tax to an
  election other than its regular one only if the council unanimously declares a fiscal emergency, so
  the measure needed every seat. The two charter questions the city had prepared, one on its civil
  service provisions and one on its accounting funds, were never moved after the tax failed, and the
  council adjourned. **The August 10 ballot-argument deadline that would have followed does not
  apply**, because there is no Glendale measure to write arguments about. The city has still posted no
  minutes for the meeting. Its next regular council meeting is Tuesday, August 11 at 6 p.m.

## Still running

- **Monterey Park** is in the ten calendar day period, which began August 5, in which anyone may read
  the filed arguments, the impartial analysis and the rest of the material on its hotel tax measure.
  The city's own timeline sets that start date, and its filing window closed on July 16. The measure
  would raise the tax paid by hotel and motel guests from 13 percent to 14 percent, and by short term
  rental guests from 13 percent to 16 percent.
