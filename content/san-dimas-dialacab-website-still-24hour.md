San Dimas cut Dial-a-Cab, the shared-ride taxi any resident can call, to 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. on September 1. On the schedule's eighth day, the city's transportation page still says the service "operates twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week."

The Pomona Valley Transportation Authority, which runs the program, lists "24 hours a day, 365 days a year" under hours of operation on its own San Dimas Dial-a-Cab page, the page the city's site links riders to.

The stakes fall on the riders the service exists for. In the authority's March evaluation, 70 percent of riders owned no vehicle and nearly half rode for medical trips; 41 riders took 1,284 after-hours trips over five months of 2025. A resident who plans a late trip from either page is planning on a cab that now stops at 8 p.m.

The council approved the shorter schedule 5-0 on July 28.
