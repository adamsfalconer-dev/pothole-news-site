A voter in Glendora Unified who thinks $86 million is too much to borrow, and one in Claremont Unified who thinks $77 million is not enough, have the same five days left to say so in the argument that prints beside the measure in the sample ballot booklet. Friday, August 14 is the last day the Los Angeles County registrar's calendar allows a written argument for or against any county, school or district measure on the November 3 ballot. After Friday the only writing left is a rebuttal, and rebuttals may be filed only by the authors of arguments for and against the measure.

City measures on this ballot each got a date from the city that placed them. School, college and county measures run instead on one line in one county document, and that line covers all of them at once: "Last day to submit arguments for or against any county, district, or school measure. Arguments may not exceed 300 words in length."

## What is being asked for

Five bond measures are on that August 14 clock, and each states on the face of its own ballot question what it would cost per $100,000 of assessed value. All five need 55 percent to pass.

**Glendora Unified** asks for $86 million, at approximately $33 per $100,000 of assessed value, about $5 million a year while the bonds are outstanding. Its question says it does this "without increasing current tax rates."

**Charter Oak Unified** asks for $75 million, at $40 per $100,000, about $5 million a year. Its rate is the highest of the five.

**Claremont Unified** asks for $77 million, at approximately $32 per $100,000, about $5 million a year, and its question also says "without increasing current taxes."

**The Valle Lindo School District** asks for $9.2 million, the smallest on the list, at approximately $29 per $100,000, generating on average $536,000 a year.

**The Pasadena Area Community College District** asks for $485 million, more than the other four together. Its question says the district would do it "without exceeding the current $9 per $100,000 assessed value (no increase)," and projects $27 million a year.

On a home assessed at $500,000, those rates work out to roughly $165 a year in Glendora Unified, $200 in Charter Oak Unified, $160 in Claremont Unified, $145 in Valle Lindo and $45 in the community college district. Assessed value is the figure the county carries on the roll, which on a long-held house sits well below what it would sell for.

Three of the five questions state a rate per $100,000 and also tell voters the bond arrives without a tax increase. What reconciles those two sentences is not on the ballot, and it is the sort of thing an argument on either side would have to explain.

A sixth measure sits on the same date and asks for no money: Hacienda La Puente Unified is asking whether board members should be "limited to serving no more than two (2) four-year terms during their lifetime regardless of the trustee area represented," on a simple majority.

One thing to know before searching the county's list. Its heading for one district reads "CHARTER OAKS UNIFIED SCHOOL DISTRICT," while the ballot question inside that same entry reads "Charter Oak Unified School District's." The second is the district's own name.

## The calendar fixes a day and not an hour

Baldwin Park's clerk fixed 4 p.m. for arguments on its city measure. San Gabriel's council resolution fixed 5 p.m. for its own. The registrar's entry for county, school and district measures gives August 14 and stops there.

That is not an artifact of a document that never states hours. The same calendar prints a 5 p.m. hour at three other entries, including the water agency nominee deadline three days later. It can print an hour and at the argument deadline it does not. Neither does the registrar's own 2026 booklet for school and district measures, which gives the deadline by date and points back to the calendar for "the applicable dates and times." So an argument filed Friday morning is safe in a way one filed at 4:55 p.m. may not be.

## Where the argument goes

That booklet names one office. Ballot argument materials, "including arguments and rebuttals, must be accompanied by a transmittal letter and DELIVERED" to the Election Planning Section, 12400 Imperial Highway, Room 2015, Norwalk, 90650, at (562) 462-2317. A faxed argument may be sent to the registrar, but "forms with original signatures must be received by the RR/CC office within 48 hours of the fax transmission." The forms come from that same section by phone, or from lavote.gov.

Two limits on that. The booklet writes its filing instructions to districts rather than to voters, and it never says who is qualified to author an argument. And the calendar carries a separate entry, dated August 6, requiring a notice published "once in a newspaper of general circulation in the district, setting forth the date fixed for submitting arguments," with a copy to the district superintendent or secretary and one posted at the district office. That notice carries the date the district itself fixed. The rule does not require it on a district website, and it is not on Glendora Unified's or the community college district's.

So a voter with 300 words ready has three things to ask the registrar's Election Coordination Unit at (562) 462-2912, the number the calendar itself gives for confirming "the correct deadline for a specific jurisdiction": where this district's arguments are filed, by what hour Friday, and whether the district's own notice fixed a different date.
