A San Gabriel voter who wants to make the case for or against the one-cent sales tax on the November 3 ballot has until 5 p.m. Tuesday to have it received by the city clerk. In Baldwin Park the same window shuts the next afternoon at 4. In South Pasadena it stays open until August 14. In Monterey Park it closed on July 16.

The dates do not land together. August 14 at 5 p.m. is the deadline in South Pasadena and in Diamond Bar, and it is also the date the county registrar's calendar fixes for county, school and district measures, with a caution attached for districts shared with another county. Rosemead's date does not appear on the city's elections page.

San Gabriel's resolution and South Pasadena's notice each cap a direct argument at 300 words and require the author's printed name and signature; San Gabriel's adds that if more than one argument is filed on a side, the city clerk selects one for and one against. Where the records set a rebuttal length, it is 250 words.

## In the cities, each city's own record carries the date

Five cities appear on the registrar's July 24 list with a measure of their own, out of 28 local measures countywide: San Gabriel, Baldwin Park, South Pasadena, Monterey Park and Rosemead. Where a city has published a notice, the notice says who inside the city fixed the date.

Baldwin Park's is the most explicit about that. It says that "based upon the time reasonably necessary to prepare and print the arguments and sample ballots for the election, the City Clerk has fixed August 5, 2026 at 4:00 p.m." as the date after which no arguments for or against the measure may be submitted. Arguments may be changed or withdrawn up to that point. Rebuttals, capped at 250 words, are due to the clerk by 4 p.m. on August 17. The measure would apply the city's existing 4 percent cannabis retailing tax to non-retail cannabis businesses, which the ballot question puts at about $1 million a year.

South Pasadena's notice, dated July 8 and signed by the chief city clerk, uses the same formula and lands on a different date: "the City Clerk has fixed Friday, August 14, 2026, at 5:00 P.M." Arguments go to City Hall at 1414 Mission Street and may be changed or withdrawn until the deadline. The city council separately authorized rebuttals of up to 250 words, to be filed by the authors of the opposing arguments within 10 days after the direct-argument deadline. The measure asks for $80 million in bonds to fix streets, roads and sidewalks, improve emergency evacuation routes and repair storm drains, at an average of $33 per $100,000 of assessed value, and needs two thirds of the vote.

San Gabriel's date is in a council resolution. Resolution 26-30, adopted July 21 and certified by the chief city clerk, says arguments "must be submitted to and received by the City Clerk by or before 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, August 4, 2026," that anything later "will not be accepted," and that nothing sent "via electronic mail or fax machine" will be accepted either. The clerk's office is at 425 S. Mission Drive. Rebuttals there are due at 5 p.m. on Friday, August 14. The measure runs on the ballot as San Gabriel Public Safety/Local Control and asks voters to approve "a 1 ¢ sales tax providing approximately $7,100,000 annually until ended by voters." The city's own staff report says a quarter-cent increase, the most the current state cap allows, would generate about $1.7 million a year, and that any amount above a quarter cent would depend on state legislation letting San Gabriel exceed the cap.

Monterey Park's window is already shut. The timeline in the staff report for its July 15 council meeting gives July 16 as the last day to file arguments and the city attorney's impartial analysis, July 26 for rebuttals, and August 5 for the start of a ten calendar day public examination period. The same timeline appears in the July 1 packet, at the meeting where the council placed the measure, along with a July 2 date to post notice of the filing deadline. The measure would raise the tax paid by hotel and motel guests from 13 percent to 14 percent, and by short term rental guests from 13 percent to 16 percent, which the city puts at about $600,000 a year.

No argument deadline for Rosemead's measure appeared on the city's pages when they were checked. Its charter measure is on the county's list, and the city clerk's elections page carries the notice of election, the resolution placing the measure, posted as a scanned image, and a notice of who has pulled nomination papers. Checked on July 31, neither that page nor the city's public notices page names a date after which arguments may no longer be filed, and no separate notice of one appears elsewhere on the city's site. Rosemead's clerk's office is at 8838 East Valley Boulevard, open Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m., and can be reached at 626-569-2100 or cityclerk@rosemeadca.gov.

## Two more valley cities have a measure of their own

Diamond Bar's council adopted a resolution on July 21 placing a 1 percent local sales tax on the same ballot, projected at about $6 million a year. The measure is not on the registrar's July 24 list. That resolution, No. 2026-28, carries its own deadline: direct arguments "shall be filed with the City Clerk on or before August 14, 2026," and rebuttals of up to 250 words on or before August 24. The staff report puts the hour at 5 p.m.

Chino Hills has a transactions and use tax measure and votes through San Bernardino County rather than Los Angeles County. On San Bernardino County's list of November measures, the entry for Chino Hills reads: "Contact the Chino Hills City Clerk's Office for argument filing deadlines." This story does not have that date.

## Getting it there, and who may answer it

Of the four cities with a published deadline still ahead, only San Gabriel's record says how an argument may not be delivered. Resolution 26-30 says arguments "received via electronic mail or fax machine will not be accepted," and puts the clerk's office at 425 S. Mission Drive.

The other three say where, or when, and not how. Baldwin Park's notice gives no street address and no method, and says arguments are due to the clerk "during normal office hours as posted"; the city's website gives City Hall as 14403 E. Pacific Avenue and its hours as 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Monday through Thursday, closed Friday, Saturday and Sunday. South Pasadena's notice says arguments "shall be filed with the City Clerk at City Hall, 1414 Mission Street." Diamond Bar's resolution says direct arguments "shall be filed with the City Clerk" and points to filing guidelines "available in the Office of the City Clerk," which the city puts at 21810 Copley Drive. None of those three records says whether email is accepted, and none of them says it is not. This story cannot tell you which it is.

The rebuttal dates are not open to everyone, and this is the part the dates alone will not tell you. San Gabriel's resolution and Diamond Bar's Resolution No. 2026-28 use nearly the same words: the author or a majority of the authors of an argument may prepare and submit a rebuttal, or may authorize someone else in writing to do it. South Pasadena's notice says rebuttals are "to be filed by the authors of opposing direct arguments." Baldwin Park's says that if arguments are submitted by the deadline, "the authors of said arguments will have an opportunity to submit a rebuttal argument."

## The county, the schools and the districts run on one date

Measures belonging to the county itself, to a school district or to another special district appear on a calendar the Los Angeles County registrar publishes for the whole election. That calendar fixes Friday, August 14 as the last day to submit arguments "for or against any county, district, or school measure," capped at 300 words. Rebuttals are due Monday, August 24, capped at 250. The calendar attaches a note to that date: deadlines for districts shared with another county may differ.

Four more measures on the registrar's list fall under that date. The Hacienda La Puente Unified School District is asking whether board members should be limited to two consecutive four year terms in a lifetime, "regardless of the trustee area represented." Charter Oak Unified is asking for $75 million in bonds and Valle Lindo School District for $9.2 million. Los Angeles County, countywide, is asking voters to amend the county charter to establish a binding arbitration process for resolving certain labor disputes, covering, in the ballot question's words, employees "including firefighters, sheriffs, district attorney investigators, medical examiners, and lifeguards," along with their supervisors and nonadministrative civilian employees in the fire, sheriff and medical examiner departments.

The calendar also sets Thursday, August 6 as the date by which a notice naming that deadline must be published once in a newspaper of general circulation in the district. Anyone who wants to confirm the deadline for a specific jurisdiction, it says, may contact the Election Coordination Unit at (562) 462-2912.

## After the deadline there is one more window

Between August 15 and August 24, the registrar has to make the measure text, the arguments, any fiscal impact statement, any impartial analysis and any bond tax rate statement available for anyone to read. During those ten days, the calendar says, "any person may file a writ of mandate or an injunction" to have the material amended or deleted. The cities run their own version of the same window: South Pasadena's notice promises ten calendar days of public examination after its own filing deadline, and Monterey Park's timeline sets a ten calendar day period beginning August 5.
