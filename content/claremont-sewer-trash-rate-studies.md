A Claremont household with a 64-gallon trash cart pays the city $46.57 a month today for sewer collection, refuse and street sweeping. Under the rates the City Council will consider at a public hearing on Tuesday, September 22, that becomes $76.50 by July 2030, an increase of $29.93 a month, or about $359 a year.

The city has mailed a Proposition 218 notice with those numbers to every sewer and sanitation customer and property owner. It is the notice, not the hearing, that starts the clock a ratepayer can act on.

A protest has to be on paper. The notice says written protests must be mailed or hand delivered to the City Clerk at 207 Harvard Avenue, Claremont, California 91711, and received before the hearing begins at 6:30 p.m. on September 22, or handed to the clerk at the hearing before public comment closes. "Protests submitted via e-mail or other electronic means will not be accepted," it reads. Each one has to say the owner or customer of record opposes the change, give the street address or Assessor's Parcel Number, and carry a name and a signature. The notice asks that the envelope be marked as a protest on the proposed sewer and sanitation rate changes.

Speaking is not protesting. "Oral comments at the Public Hearing will not qualify as formal protests unless accompanied by a written protest," the notice says. It also sets the threshold: "If written protests are filed by a majority of the affected parcels, the proposed rates will not be adopted," and "while both property owners and customers of record may submit written protests, only one protest will be counted for each parcel."

Sewer is the steepest increase in percentage terms. The monthly charge for a single-family residence, now $6.63 per dwelling unit, becomes $7.96 on January 1, then rises each July 1 to $9.55, $11.46, $12.89 and $14.50 by 2030. That is a 119 percent increase, from annual adjustments of 20 percent three years running and 12.5 percent twice. Apartments, condominiums, duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes and planned unit developments are billed on the same per dwelling unit basis.

Trash and street sweeping rise about 55 percent from a higher base, on adjustments of 10 percent for three years and 8 percent for two. A 35-gallon cart goes from $29.56 to $45.90 a month, a 64-gallon cart from $35.32 to $54.83 and a 96-gallon cart from $45.06 to $69.96. Street sweeping for a single-family home goes from $4.62 to $7.17. A commercial customer with one bin collected once a week goes from $182.32 a month to $283.05. The full schedule for every cart size, unit count and pickup frequency is at claremontca.gov/sanitation_rate.

The mailed notice prints a different address. The footnote under its sanitation table sends readers to www.claremontca.org/sanitation_rate, ending in.org, and that address returns a "not found" error. The city's domain is claremontca.gov, and www.claremontca.gov/sanitation_rate reaches the page, which carries the notice, the July 28 staff report and the presentation the council saw.

The city's stated reason is a raise it did not take. "Although annual rate adjustments were adopted in 2021, the City did not implement a rate adjustment in the first year of the current financial plan," the notice says, and inflation and contract costs kept moving in the meantime. Sewage treatment happens downstream at the Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts, which bills separately, so this line is not the whole cost.

The rate studies behind the notice were prepared by Willdan and dated July 21. Neither fund is in trouble today. The sanitation study says the aim "was not to address an immediate funding shortfall, but rather to evaluate alternative approaches for restoring long-term revenue sufficiency," and the sewer study uses the same construction. What is projected to change is cost. The sewer study also notes that a 20 percent first-year adjustment "equates to approximately $1.33 per month for a typical single-family residential customer." By the last year in the same table, the monthly increase over today is $7.87.

One body has already voted. The Community and Human Services Commission recommended the package 4 to 0 on July 15. Its minutes record that no written public comment had been received on the item and that nobody asked to speak.
