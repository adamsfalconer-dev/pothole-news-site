Monterey Park spent more than three months shopping for a company to run its buses, its on-demand shuttle and its dial-a-ride service under one contract. On Aug. 19 the city council rejected every bid it got, including the one its own public works director recommended, and told staff to come back with options.

Rather than sign anything, the council authorized what the minutes call an "extension of current contract on a month to month basis." The minutes do not say whose contract that is. What the city's own staff report says is that First Transit, the company whose bid the council was rejecting, "is the City's current contractor for the City's Spirit Bus service."

The item was set up to be routine. It sat on the consent calendar, the block of items a council approves in one motion without separate debate, as item 10.B. It came off. The minutes record the council approving everything else on that calendar "except Item No. 10B which was pulled," then record the vote on 10.B by itself.

The public works director put out a request for proposals on May 7. It covered all three ways the city moves people: Spirit Bus, the fixed-route service; MPK Transit Express, the app-based on-demand shuttle the council authorized as a one-year pilot in November 2024; and dial-a-ride, the ride service the city runs itself. Bidders could propose on one service or all three. All four proposed on all three.

Four proposals came in by June 18. Over four years, Parking Company of America asked $10,905,207, First Transit asked $12,534,987, RTW Management asked $14,343,457, and Crosby Transit asked $19,728,341.

Two of the four were never eligible to win. The staff report says the RTW Management and Parking Company of America proposals "were deemed non-responsive because they did not include required information," so they were not considered for award, though staff read them anyway "for comparative purposes." Paperwork knocked out the cheapest bid in the room. Parking Company of America asked about $1.6 million less than the bid staff recommended, across the same four years, and was never in the running for the award.

That left First Transit as the staff pick at $12,534,987, with an initial four-year term and up to two optional one-year extensions the city alone could trigger. The staff report makes an affirmative case for it: best value on technical qualifications and cost, references checked and positive, and, unlike two of its rivals, all required documents submitted. Estimated cost for the first year, $3,003,430. The annual cost also covers shuttles for Lunar New Year, Cherry Blossom and other city events. The money for year one is in the current budget.

The minutes do not say why the council rejected the bids. They are action minutes, which record what a body did rather than what it said, and they are the city's official account of that meeting.

Item 10.B is the most heavily annotated item of the night. It carries a three-part instruction, a notation about the voting, and two roll calls, and no reason in any of it.

The two roll calls are a correction, and the minutes say so. Both were moved by Council Member Thomas Wong and seconded by Council Member Elizabeth Yang. The minutes mark the first one "Vote cast in error, revote," and in it Wong, who made the motion, is recorded in the No column. In the second, all five members are recorded in Aye. The result on the record is "Approved."

No route is being cut and no rider has to do anything. The council's action was built to keep the service moving while the city starts over: reject the bids, extend the existing contract month to month, and have staff return with options. How long that can run is not something the record answers. What it gave up in the process is a four-year price ceiling of $12,534,987 and a fixed term, and what it postponed is the plan to put Spirit Bus, the shuttle and dial-a-ride under a single operator.

The council set no date to take the question back up, and its next regular meeting came and went on Sept. 2 with no transit item on it.
