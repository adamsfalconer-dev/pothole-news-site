A 210-unit rental project at Riverside Drive and Mountain Avenue is approved in Chino. The city council voted 5-0 on Sept. 1 to grant, in part, BCT Development's appeal of a 3-3 planning commission vote that had left the project neither approved nor denied since June 17, according to draft minutes released ahead of Tuesday's council meeting.

The builder did not get everything. Both it and Warmington Residential, whose 100-unit for-sale project at Riverside and Magnolia Avenue was also before the council, asked to drop a condition requiring them to bury existing overhead utility lines, a concession they argued state density-bonus law allows for affordable projects. The council denied both requests 5-0, and denied Warmington's appeal outright. Mayor Eunice Ulloa noted the city had already granted that project eight concessions.

The two projects together total 310 units on one corridor, for renters at Mountain Avenue and buyers at Magnolia. The hearings drew letters from the state housing department and from building-industry and housing-law groups, and the city attorney said the threat of litigation in BCT's correspondence required him in any related meetings.
