The Glendale City Council voted Tuesday, Sept. 1, to mail the legal notices for a five-year water rate increase, which starts the protest process that can block it.

The vote did not set the rates. Proposition 218 is the rule in the state constitution that lets the people who pay for a service block a price increase, and it runs on a schedule: the city mails a notice, holds a hearing, and counts written protests. Tuesday's vote authorized the mailing. The hearing is set for 6 p.m. on Tuesday, Nov. 3, in the council chambers at 613 E. Broadway, and the protest window closes when that hearing ends.

One line in the notice cuts against the usual assumption about who gets a say. A written protest may be filed by the record owner of a property or by a tenant, on any parcel that receives city water. Renters are not shut out. But only one protest per parcel is counted, and the notice does not say which one the city counts if an owner and a tenant on the same parcel both send one in. The notice itself goes to owners listed in the county assessor's database and to the city's electric and water account holders, which means a tenant in a building billed through a single master meter is on neither list and will not get one in the mail. A tenant in that position can still file, and the rules for how are below.

The city is strict about the form, and most of the ways a person would ordinarily complain do not count. A protest has to be on paper. It has to say plainly that the writer opposes the rates, name the record owner or the customer of record, identify the property by street address, assessor's parcel number or account number, and carry an original signature with the name printed legibly. It can be handed in at the chambers before the hearing closes, or mailed or delivered to the city clerk at the same address, but it has to arrive before the hearing ends. There are no late protests. The notice is blunt about the rest: "[a]n electronic (emailed or faxed) or spoken protest CANNOT be counted," and "[g]eneral comments and emails are not counted as formal legal protests."

The increase is stopped outright only if a majority of the parcels that receive water service, half plus one, send in a protest, and the notice does not say how many parcels that is. Falling short of that number does not end the matter. The council still has to hold the hearing, still has to take spoken and written comment there, and after it can lower the proposed rates, though it cannot raise them past what the notice says. The four community meetings scheduled in October are the other places to be heard.

Two additions were accepted into the motion before the vote, according to the meeting recording. They were taken as friendly amendments, the ordinary way a motion is adjusted on the floor, and no one objected when the chair asked by a show of hands. The first asks staff to build the city's conservation and cost-saving programs into the four community meetings already scheduled on the rates, and to walk people through their options in person rather than leaving brochures on a table. The second asks for that outreach in more than one language and on social media, and for a report and discussion on the programs to come back to the council before the November hearing.

The item took the council more than two hours, and for now that recording is the only account of it. What the council decided will not appear in a certified written record for some time. The most recent Glendale council meeting with minutes posted is June 23, and those minutes went up on Aug. 26, 64 days later. No council meeting since has minutes. Until minutes for Tuesday appear, a resident deciding whether to protest has the video and nothing else.

The rate schedule is in the draft notice attached to Tuesday's item. If adopted, the rates take effect Jan. 1, 2027, and step up again each Jan. 1 through 2031. The notice also discloses a separate charge, set in the city's municipal code, that moves the water bill with the cost of buying water and the electricity to pump it. That one moves without another vote.

**What's next:**

Four community meetings on the rates are set for Oct. 7, 15, 17 and 24. The notice gives a time for each location but does not say which meeting falls on which date: the Adult Recreation Center and the Sparr Heights Community Center run 6 to 7:30 p.m., the Brand Park Library and Arts Center runs 9 to 10:30 a.m., and the Zoom session, which requires registration, runs 10 to 11:30 a.m. GlendaleCA.gov/ProposedWaterRates and (855) 550-4497 are the city's contacts for the rate proposal.

The public hearing is 6 p.m. Nov. 3 at 613 E. Broadway. A written protest counts only if it is on paper and either delivered to the city clerk or handed in at the hearing before it closes.
