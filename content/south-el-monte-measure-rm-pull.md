South El Monte Mayor Gloria Olmos has placed one line on Tuesday's council agenda: "Reconsider removing Measure RM from the ballot." The county's last day to accept a withdrawal request for a measure already on the November 3 ballot was August 12, 20 days before the council will take the question up.

Measure RM is the measure that would end the office Olmos holds. The ballot question asks South El Monte voters whether to amend the municipal code "to return to five elected City Council members and a Mayor selected from among the five elected City Council members." The July resolution that placed it on the ballot describes it as a measure to revert the elective office of mayor to an appointive position selected by the council from among its members. A yes vote would not cut the current term short: under the ordinance behind the measure, the mayor serving now finishes her term, which expires in November 2028, and the seat appears on that year's ballot as a council seat. From then on, South El Monte's five council members would pick the mayor from among themselves. A simple majority decides it.

Whether that question stays in front of voters is what Tuesday's item asks to reopen. The county Registrar-Recorder's calendar for this election set August 12 as the last day for the county elections official "to receive a resolution from a legislative body requesting to withdraw or amend a measure previously submitted for placement on the ballot," citing Elections Code section 9605. The county's final list of measures appearing on the ballot, revised August 20, carries both South El Monte measures, word for word: RM and Measure CL, a separate term limits question. Vote-by-mail ballots for the whole county start going out October 5.

The item reaches the council bare. In the meeting's 338-page packet there is no staff report, no cost figure and no legal analysis for it. The only staff report under the mayor's agenda heading covers her other item, a $2,000 reimbursement for a produce stand at July's Night Market. No document before the council says what a vote to remove the measure could now accomplish.

The fight over RM is already in writing. It is the only South El Monte measure with an argument in favor, an argument against, and rebuttals in both directions on file at the city's elections page; the county's deadlines for those filings passed too, August 14 for arguments and August 24 for rebuttals.

The same agenda sends the council into closed session on the case the agenda lists as Gloria Olmos, an individual, plaintiff, versus the City of South El Monte, the lawsuit the mayor has brought against the city she leads. In March the council censured Olmos and suspended her access to public funds. She signed both resolutions and wrote "I Don't agree" beneath her signature.

For a South El Monte voter, the practical questions are narrower. The county's final list of ballot measures includes RM. Anyone who wants to tell the council what to do with Tuesday's item can do it in person at 6 p.m. at 1415 Santa Anita Avenue, five minutes per speaker, or in writing to sem.cityclerk@soelmonte.org by 5 p.m. that day, capped at 250 words. Written comments enter the record but are not read aloud.
