Glendale has cancelled the August 4 City Council meeting that the city's own staff report names as the last day it can put charter amendments on the November 3 ballot. The next regular meeting is August 11, a week after that date.

The charter is the city's governing document, and the council cannot change it on its own. Voters have to approve any amendment, which is what the deadline is about.

Between now and August 4 the council has one session on its calendar: a special meeting at 4 p.m. today, in the Council Chambers at 613 E. Broadway. Its agenda went online Wednesday afternoon, about 27 hours before the meeting it calls.

Today's meeting is not the vote. The single item asks the council for "Discussion and Direction to Staff to Prepare Necessary Legislation for Council Consideration" on two things at once: a quarter-cent sales tax, and a package of charter changes. Direction to staff means the measures themselves would be drafted and brought back to a later meeting for the council to act on. Nothing on the city's published calendar puts that later meeting before August 4.

## What is riding on it

The tax is a quarter of a percent added to what shoppers pay on taxable purchases in Glendale, or 25 cents on a $100 purchase. The agenda describes it as a "Transaction and Use Tax Measure for Public Safety and City Services." Two days ago it was only a request: on the July 28 agenda it sat under the council's discussion of future agenda requests, as Mayor Kassakhian asking his colleagues to consider putting a quarter-cent sales tax to voters. Today it is the business of a noticed meeting.

The charter half covers four subjects: the city's civil service system, the charter's rules for how the city sorts and reports the money it holds, term limits and pay for the elected City Clerk and City Treasurer, and what the agenda calls miscellaneous cleanup. Inside the civil service piece are changes to how job candidates are ranked and appointed, how long new employees stay on probation, and whether department heads hired after the November election would serve at will, meaning they could be fired without cause.

The August 4 date is the city's own, and it applies to the charter half. It appears in a July 21 staff report attached to the July 28 agenda item, which says the council "must do so no later than August 4, 2026" if it wants charter amendments on the November ballot. The report was written by City Attorney Michael J. Garcia and approved by City Manager Roubik R. Golanian. In all 238 pages of the July 28 packet that date appears exactly once, no document in it sets any date for the sales tax, and none of them says what becomes of the amendments if the date passes.

## The date was not on the list

Glendale calls off council meetings routinely, and the timing of this notice carries no signal. Eight regular council meetings have been cancelled in 2026, each notice going online five or six days ahead. The August 4 notice went up six days ahead, exactly the city's ordinary lead time. It is two sentences long and gives no reason.

What makes this one different is that the council had already chosen which meetings it was giving up. On June 23 it adopted its calendar for the year ahead and cancelled seven Tuesdays outright: July 7, August 18, September 8, October 13, November 24, December 22 and December 29. August 4 was not among them. It is the first regular meeting the council has called off since that vote that it had not already cancelled in advance.

## The council is not out of options

The city's own meeting-schedule report says that if additional meetings become necessary, the council can add them to the calendar or call special ones. Nothing stops it from calling a second special meeting before August 4 and adopting the measures there, and this afternoon's session shows how fast that can happen: the agenda was typed Wednesday for a Thursday meeting.

The city has not published any meeting at which that legislation would come back.
