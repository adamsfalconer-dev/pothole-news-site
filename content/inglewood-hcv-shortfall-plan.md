The Inglewood Housing Authority is scheduled Tuesday afternoon to adopt a rewritten rulebook for its Section 8 voucher program. It raises the minimum a household must pay each month from nothing to $50, writes into policy restrictions on new vouchers the Authority says it has already imposed, and gives the Authority a power its current rules deny it: cutting the rent subsidy for families who are already under contract.

The Housing Authority is the Inglewood City Council. The agenda says the council members serve in that capacity for the Successor Agency, Housing Authority, Finance Authority, Parking Authority and Joint Powers Authority. Tuesday's agenda carries four of them, the City Council, the Successor Agency, the Housing Authority and the Parking Authority, meeting in sequence in one open session that begins at 2 p.m. The Housing Authority's calendar, which carries item H-2, comes after the City Council's and the Successor Agency's.

## What changes for a household

The clearest change is a number. The plan Inglewood has operated under since July 1, 2025 states, in the policy line that sets it, that "the minimum rent for this locality is $0." In the draft up for adoption Tuesday, the same line reads $50. That is the ceiling federal rules allow. Both versions of the plan print the governing range: "A minimum rent between $0 and $50 that is established by the PHA," the public housing agency, which here is the Inglewood Housing Authority itself.

The staff report describes the change as raising the minimum total tenant payment to $50, which it calls "the maximum amount permitted under applicable federal regulations," unless a household is granted a hardship exemption. The summary of changes attached to the item puts it as "Establish a minimum tenant rent of $50." The total tenant payment is what the rent formula produces; the minimum rent is the floor written into that formula.

That formula takes whichever of four figures is highest. Two of them are income-based: 30 percent of a household's adjusted monthly income, and 10 percent of its gross income. The third is the welfare rent, which the plan says does not apply in Inglewood. The fourth is the minimum rent. A household whose income-based figures come out under $50, which today can mean zero, would owe $50.

The way out is a hardship exemption, and the plan sets out how to ask. A family has to request one in writing, explaining the nature of the hardship and how it has affected the family's ability to pay. The Authority must suspend the minimum rent starting the first of the month after the request arrives, and must decide within 30 calendar days. The five qualifying circumstances are federal ones: losing or awaiting a decision on a government assistance program, facing eviction for being unable to pay the minimum rent, a drop in income from changed family circumstances including job loss, a death in the family, and any additional criteria the Authority sets, of which the plan says it has established none.

A granted exemption is not always the end of it. The plan defines a hardship expected to last 90 days or less as temporary. In that case the minimum rent is suspended for 90 days and the family must then resume paying it and repay what was suspended. If the Authority finds no hardship, it reinstates the minimum rent and requires the family to repay the suspended amount within 30 calendar days.

## New vouchers, already restricted

The restrictions on new vouchers are not something Tuesday's vote starts. The staff report says the Authority "has significantly restricted the issuance of new vouchers," with exceptions where issuance is required by federal regulation, a court order, portability obligations, Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing referrals and other mandatory special-purpose programs. It says the Authority "has suspended consideration of new Project-Based Voucher (PBV) commitments and additional PBV funding obligations," apart from existing contractual commitments and projects the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development determines are needed for public housing repositioning. Project-based vouchers are attached to particular units rather than carried by a household.

What Tuesday's vote does is write those restrictions into the rulebook that governs the program.

## The line that reaches people who already have a voucher

The current plan gives families under contract a guarantee. If the Authority lowers its payment standard schedule, the adopted plan says, it "will not reduce the payment standard used to calculate subsidy for families under HAP contract as long as the HAP contract remains in effect." A HAP contract is the housing assistance payment contract between the Authority and the landlord, the agreement that pays the subsidy portion of the rent.

The draft keeps that sentence and adds one after it, in the draft's own wording: "However, if the PHA is in shortfall status, the PHA may implement cost-saving measures described in PIH notice 2025-28. Cost-savings measures includes immediately reducing Payment Standards to the basic range." PIH notices are the federal housing department's guidance to public housing agencies; the Inglewood documents cite two of them, 2025-28 and 2026-12, as the authority for these measures.

The payment standard is the ceiling the Authority uses to work out how much of a household's rent it will cover. It is the reason the guarantee in the current plan exists at all: a lower ceiling produces a smaller subsidy for the same unit, so the current plan holds the old ceiling in place for the length of a family's contract.

The schedule itself has already been revised, and one tier of it dropped. The Authority's payment standards sheet is dated effective June 24, 2026, nearly three months before Tuesday's vote. It says that because of HUD's June 10 notification, the Authority "is implementing cost-saving measures outlined in PIH Notice 2026-12, including adjustments to Payment Standards," that it has revised the standards to HUD's Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code, the local rent benchmarks HUD publishes, and that it is "discontinuing the use of Exception Payment Standards, except when approved as a reasonable accommodation in accordance with applicable HUD requirements." The sheet says the standards "were set at 110% of the 1/1/26 safmrs for cost savings measures imposed by HUD," using the abbreviation for those same fair market rents.

Under the plan now in force, that lowered schedule does not reach a family already under contract. That is the protection Tuesday's language would qualify.

## Why now

On June 10, 2026, the staff report says, the Authority received a Funding Shortfall Projection Notice from HUD identifying it as a Group 2 Shortfall Agency. The draft resolution's recitals say the same. The payment standards sheet carries the same date, citing "HUD's June 10, 2026 notification regarding funding limitations." Under HUD guidance, the report says, agencies designated this way must implement reasonable cost-saving measures and monitor spending to stay eligible for shortfall assistance funding.

A letter from Chairman James T. Butts Jr. to HUD's Los Angeles field office spells out what the designation means. It marks the Authority, the letter says, "as a Group 2 Public Housing Agency projected to experience a Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) funding shortfall during Calendar Year 2026," and it "represents the Authority's fourth confirmed shortfall since 2016." The letter is dated August 25, 2026. The third thing item H-2 asks the board to do Tuesday is authorize the Chairman to execute and transmit it.

The measures may not be enough, by the Authority's own account. Current projections, the staff report says, "indicate that the IHA remains at risk of a funding shortfall due to continued rent inflation and rising housing assistance costs in the local rental market," using the Authority's initials. It says the steps taken show that all reasonable measures are being implemented "before consideration of more severe actions, including participant terminations or termination of Project-Based Voucher HAP contracts," and that such actions "would be considered only as a last resort if sufficient funding is unavailable to sustain current program operations." The report says there is no impact to the city's General Fund.

What the board is asked to adopt Tuesday is the 913-page draft plan itself. The resolution says it "shall replace and shall supersede all prior versions of the Administrative Plan," and that it takes effect immediately on adoption. The draft's cover page reads "Effective Sept. 15, 2026."

## What's next

The Housing Authority takes up item H-2 on Tuesday, September 15. Closed session begins at 1 p.m. and open session at 2 p.m., in the City Council Chamber. The agenda says chamber doors open at 1:30 p.m. for open session and that seating is first come, first served.

The agenda gives two deadlines for accommodations, and they are different. A disability-related modification or accommodation, including a translation service, to observe the meeting or offer public comment may be requested from the City Clerk's office no later than 24 hours before the meeting. Requests for special accommodations due to a disability, the agenda says separately, must be received 72 hours before the day of the meeting. The City Clerk's office is at One Manchester Boulevard.

The full draft plan is posted on the Authority's Administrative Plan page, alongside the version it would replace.
