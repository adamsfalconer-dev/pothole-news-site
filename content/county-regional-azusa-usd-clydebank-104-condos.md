Los Angeles County decides Wednesday morning whether a closed elementary school campus on Clydebank Avenue, an unincorporated pocket with a Covina address, becomes 104 condominiums of up to two stories each. The 8.5-acre site belongs to the Azusa Unified School District, and the project goes before the county's Regional Planning Commission at 9 a.m. as item 11 on its agenda.

No decision has been made yet: the commission can approve the project, deny it, or put it off.

The plan, filed by applicant Tim Sales with the district as owner, would demolish the closed school buildings and put up 104 detached condo units, each up to two stories with an attached two-car garage, plus 33 guest parking spaces. County code would normally allow 77 units on the site. The project reaches 104 through a 35 percent density bonus under county housing rules, granted in exchange for 16 of the homes being set aside for sale at the lower income level, 80 percent of area median income. The bonus package also lets the builder reduce setbacks, building separation, landscaping and other standards the county would otherwise require, and the work would move 167,894 cubic yards of dirt and remove two oak trees.

Neighbors are organized against it. Resident Christopher Kearney, writing to the commission on Aug. 26 "along with the property owners and community members," asked it to "deny, or at a minimum postpone approval" of the project his letter calls "The Cove at Clydebank." The letter, included in the county's own hearing materials, argues the surrounding streets cannot take the traffic, that the traffic study relies on data three to four years old from when the school was still open, and that with estimated sale prices around $1 million the project "does nothing for the affordability issue." It also asks for "a complete and current environmental impact report." The county has determined no new environmental study is required, citing an exemption in state environmental law, CEQA Section 15183. A second letter, from Frances S. Carothers on Tudor Street, names the site as the former Alice M. Ellington School and says its schoolyard has served the neighborhood as open space for over 50 years, a loss she writes the project's small interior park will not make up for.

The project also has organized support. The county's Sept. 1 supplemental report lists four comments in favor, from the housing-advocacy organizations Californians For Homeownership, CalHDF and YIMBY Law plus one resident, and two opposed, from the Covina Coalition and one resident. The applicant held community meetings on May 27 and July 8 and, as of this week, is still asking the county to change several conditions of approval, including one on required exterior materials.

For the households on Clydebank Avenue, Tudor Street and Aspan Avenue, the three streets that would serve the project, Wednesday's hearing is the county's decision point on whether a closed campus becomes 104 new households, and whether 16 below-market homes get built in a neighborhood where the opposition letter says new floor plans would be double the size of surrounding houses.

Residents can weigh in before the vote. The commission meets at 9 a.m. Wednesday, Sept. 2, in Room 150 at 320 W. Temple St. in downtown Los Angeles, with remote comment by Zoom at bit.ly/ZOOM-RPC (webinar ID 858 6032 6429) or by phone at (669) 444-9171.
