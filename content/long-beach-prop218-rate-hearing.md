Long Beach Utilities' proposed increases for the coming year, 6 percent on water rates and 8 percent on sewer, reach their Proposition 218 hearing at 7 tonight at the utilities administration building, 1800 East Wardlow Road. By the department's own arithmetic, the two together add $5.74 a month to the average single-family bill: $4.64 of it water, $1.10 sewer.

For a ratepayer who wants the increases stopped, tonight is the deadline that matters, and the rules are strict. Only a written protest counts, signed by hand by the account holder, with a name and a street address, an assessor's parcel number, or an account number. Electronic signatures are not accepted. One protest is counted per account, and a tenant who pays the water bill directly can file one, not just the property owner. A protest can be emailed as a completed scan to commission@lbutilities.org, mailed or hand-delivered to the executive assistant to the board at 1800 East Wardlow Road, or brought to the hearing itself, where blank forms will be available. Whatever the route, it must be received before the hearing closes. Speaking during public comment does not count as a protest unless a signed one is turned in too.

The counting happens right away. The board's executive assistant tabulates protests as soon as the hearing closes and reports the result. If a majority of affected ratepayers file valid written protests, the board cannot adopt the increases. Short of a majority, the hearing concludes and the rates move to the City Council, which considers them September 8.

The increases come out of the budget the utilities board adopted June 4, after three budget planning sessions in May and June. A 2 percent natural gas increase for residential customers is in the same budget, but gas is not part of tonight's hearing; Proposition 218 reaches water and sewer. The department says the money funds a $62.9 million water capital program and a $35.9 million sewer program in the coming budget year, including rehabilitating the 1950s-era 32nd Street booster pump station and replacing aging sewer force mains in the beach and marina areas, a project it expects to finish in 2028.

One door has already closed. Under a state law that took effect in January 2025, only a ratepayer who filed a timely written objection may later sue over these rates, and those objections were due August 20. The department received 203 of them. In a staff letter on tonight's agenda, general manager B. Anatole Falagán writes that the objections do not warrant clarifying the rates, reducing them, or reviewing them further, and recommends the board proceed. For those who did object, a court challenge must come within 120 days of the rates taking effect or their final approval, whichever is later.

A household already behind on its bill has somewhere to start: the department's line for setting up a payment plan on an outstanding balance is (562) 570-5700, and it also offers a one-time Household Water Assistance Program and free leak repairs for income-eligible homeowners through Rebuilding Together Long Beach.
