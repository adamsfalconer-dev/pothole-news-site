La Verne's City Council has chosen who gets to decide whether the fee that pays for the city's paramedics more than doubles, and it is not the city's voters.

The council voted 5-0 at its July 20 meeting to direct staff to begin a Proposition 218 assessment process to raise the Measure GG paramedic service fee to $14 a month from $6.90, according to the city's recording of the meeting. That is a rise from about $82.80 a year to about $168, roughly $85 more, collected on residents' water and refuse bills. The vote starts the process. It does not set the fee, and the increase can still fail.

Proposition 218 is the 1996 state measure that requires cities to notice and ballot property owners before imposing or raising an assessment on real property. Whether it applied here was the question the council had been weighing for months, against the alternative of putting the increase to voters on November 3. The staff report recommends the assessment route on the city attorney's analysis that the charge, although voters created it in 1980, is an assessment governed by Article XIII D of the California Constitution, which does not require a vote of the registered electorate. The assessment is not imposed merely to provide paramedic staffing or ambulance availability to the community generally, the report says. It purchases a specific financial benefit for each assessed property: people covered through that property are not personally responsible for deductibles, copayments or uninsured balances when city paramedics treat or transport them.

That distinction moves the decision, and it also separates the people who decide from many of the people who pay. The fee is collected on utility bills, which reach whoever holds the account, renters included. The ballots go to record owners of parcels, one per affected parcel. The report says the ballots are counted by weight rather than by head, and it does not say how the weighting is set. It cites Government Code section 53753, which provides that an assessment ballot proceeding is not an election under the Elections Code, and states plainly that the voting is not one person, one vote.

Nothing mails yet. Under the process the report lays out, the city first prepares a fee study and a parcel list, then the council must take a second action to start the proceedings, accept the study, approve the rate structure and authorize the mailing before any notice or ballot goes out. Owners would then get a notice and a ballot at least 45 days before a public hearing, and the city clerk opens and tabulates the ballots in a publicly accessible place once the hearing closes. The increase cannot be levied if the ballots submitted in opposition outweigh those submitted in favor.

The gap the city says it is closing is wide. The report puts current fee revenue at about $1,059,840 a year and insurance collections for ambulance and emergency medical care at roughly $1.7 million, about $2.8 million together, against paramedic and emergency transportation costs it puts at a minimum of about $3.8 million, a figure it calls outdated and low. At $14 a month the fee would raise about $2,150,400, which with the same insurance collections would roughly close that gap.

The part with no end date is the escalator. Measure GG's original annual adjustment was tied to the general Los Angeles-area consumer price index, and the report's complaint is that it let costs outrun the revenue meant to cover them. In its place the report proposes an adjustment set 80 percent by a fire personnel cost index and 20 percent by the medical care services index, both of which track what the city pays for firefighters and medical care rather than what households pay for everything. If owners approve it, they approve a fee designed to rise faster every year than the one it replaces, permanently.

Residents who go looking for the city's own account of this will not find it yet. As of July 28, La Verne's Measure GG page still describes the council as considering whether to move ahead with a potential ballot measure, and lists the current charge at $6.90.

La Verne is still on the November 3 ballot. The same meeting adopted the resolutions calling the city's general municipal election and asking the county to consolidate and run it. The paramedic fee is simply not on it.
