Rolling Hills Estates is set to take its final vote Tuesday on Ordinance No. 764, its first rules specifically for e-bikes and motorized devices on city streets and paved paths.

It would rewrite two chapters of the municipal code. E-bike, moped, scooter, hoverboard and powered-unicycle riders would have to yield to pedestrians, carry a passenger only on a separate attached seat, and not tow, stunt-ride or race without city permission.

On horse trails it would ban all non-horse-drawn wheeled vehicles, from bicycles and skateboards to strollers and wagons. Exceptions cover adjacent residents moving horse-keeping equipment, feed and supplies, and construction deliveries that cannot reach a property any other way.

The council introduced the ordinance 4-0 on Sept. 8 with no public testimony, draft minutes record. Since then a resident, writing for two neighbors too, has asked in a letter in the council's packet for a reasonableness standard in the horse-keeping carve-out, arguing it does not adequately protect owners along the Palomino Ranchos Trail, which he says is private property under a city easement.
